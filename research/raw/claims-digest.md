
## Discrete diffusion theory & foundations — lens: What / mechanisms
- [S|high|2021-07-07 (v3 2023-02-22), NeurIPS 2021 — historical relevance explicit] D3PM is the founding formalism for discrete diffusion: the forward process corrupts tokens via categorical transition matrices Q_t (proposed variants: uniform/multinomial, absorbing [MASK] state, discretized Gaussian, nearest-neighbor in embedding space); training uses a hybrid loss L_lambda = variational lower bound + auxiliary cross-entropy; the absorbing-state variant explicitly bridges diffusion with masked language models (BERT is a one-step diffusion model, per Sec. 4) and autoregressive models, and the choice of transition matrix is a first-order design decision for text vs image results (Austin, Johnson, Ho, Tarlow, van den Berg; NeurIPS 2021).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03006>
- [S|high|2021-02, NeurIPS 2021 — historical relevance explicit] Multinomial diffusion (with Argmax Flows) is the direct predecessor of D3PM: it introduced gradually adding categorical (uniform) noise to discrete data with a learned denoising process, without embedding text into continuous space, and outperformed dequantization approaches on text log-likelihood (Hoogeboom, Nielsen, Jaini, Forré, Welling; NeurIPS 2021).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05379>
- [S|high|2022-05, NeurIPS 2022] Campbell et al. gave the first continuous-time (CTMC) formulation of discrete denoising diffusion: forward and reverse processes are Continuous Time Markov Chains with rate matrices, trained by a continuous-time ELBO; sampling uses tau-leaping borrowed from chemical physics plus a predictor-corrector scheme, with a theoretical bound on the error between generated and true data distributions (NeurIPS 2022). This is the mathematical backbone reused by SEDD and later samplers.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14987>
- [S|high|2021-10, ICLR 2022] Autoregressive Diffusion Models (ARDM) proved early that order-agnostic autoregressive models and absorbing discrete diffusion are special cases of one model class: ARDMs train without causal masking using a diffusion-style objective, support budget-adaptive parallel generation, and reach 1.43 bits/char on text8 in 250 steps where discrete diffusion needed 1000 — the original seed of the 'masked diffusion = any-order AR' equivalence (Hoogeboom et al., ICLR 2022).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.02037>
- [S|high|2023-10, ICML 2024] SEDD (ICML 2024 Best Paper) extends score matching to discrete space via the concrete score — ratios p_t(y)/p_t(x) for neighboring sequences (Hamming distance 1) — trained with the score entropy loss, sum over y~x of [s_theta(x)_y − (p(y|x0)/p(x|x0)) log s_theta(x)_y] in its tractable denoising form; it is convex with a log-barrier, and SEDD reduces perplexity 25–75% vs prior diffusion LMs, e.g., SEDD Small <=41.84 on WikiText2 vs GPT-2 Small 42.43, with un-annealed generative quality ~6–8x better than un-annealed GPT-2 (Lou, Meng, Ermon).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16834>
- [A|high|2024] Aaron Lou's expository blog gives the cleanest derivation chain for score-based discrete diffusion: why continuous score matching fails on discrete data, concrete score definition, score entropy and denoising score entropy, CTMC forward equation dp_t/dt = Q p_t, and the reverse transition p(x_{t−dt}=a|x_t=b) ≈ delta_b(a) + (p_t(a)/p_t(b)) Q(b,a) dt — with the compute-for-quality log-log tradeoff of sampling iterations made explicit.  <https://aaronlou.com/blog/2024/discrete-diffusion/>
- [S|high|2024-06, NeurIPS 2024] MDLM reduces absorbing-state diffusion to a Rao-Blackwellized, schedule-invariant weighted masked-LM loss. Exact continuous-time objective from the project page: L_NELBO = E_q ∫_0^1 [alpha'_t/(1−alpha_t)] * sum_l log<x_theta^l(z_t), x^l> dt — i.e., time-weighted cross-entropy on masked tokens only. The SUBS parameterization enforces zero masking probabilities (MASK logit = −inf) and carry-over unmasking (unmasked tokens copied through). Results at 110M/327B tokens on LM1B: MDLM 23.00 PPL vs AR Transformer 20.86 vs SEDD <=32.79 (Sahoo et al., NeurIPS 2024).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07524>
- [S|high|2024-06, NeurIPS 2024] MD4 (DeepMind) independently proved the same simplification: the continuous-time variational objective of masked diffusion is a simple weighted integral of cross-entropy losses; it adds GenMD4 with state-dependent masking schedules, surpasses prior diffusion LMs at GPT-2 scale on OpenWebText (better on 4/5 zero-shot tasks), and gets 2.75 bpd on CIFAR-10 / 3.40 bpd on ImageNet 64x64 as a pixel-level discrete model, beating similar-size AR models (Shi, Han, Wang, Doucet, Titsias; NeurIPS 2024). Code: github.com/google-deepmind/md4.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04329>
- [S|high|2024-06, ICLR 2025] RADD proved the concrete score of absorbing diffusion factorizes as (time-independent conditional probability of clean data) x (analytic time-dependent scalar), so the time-conditioning input can be removed entirely; this enables caching denoiser outputs across steps (up to 3.5x faster sampling) and yields SOTA zero-shot perplexity among diffusion LMs at GPT-2 scale, while unifying the absorbing-diffusion and any-order-AR views (ML-GSAI; ICLR 2025).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03736>
- [S|high|2024-09 (rev. 2025-04), ICLR 2025] The sharpest theoretical criticism: Zheng et al. prove both training and sampling of masked diffusion models are theoretically free of the time variable — 'the key signature of diffusion models' — making them equivalent to (order-agnostic) masked models; their first-hitting sampler is exactly equivalent to MDM generation with ~20x speedup; and they expose a float32 Gumbel categorical-sampling numerical bug that silently lowers effective sampling temperature, reducing token diversity and making prior MDM 'beats AR generative perplexity' evaluations 'somewhat unfair' (ICLR 2025).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02908>
- [S|medium|2025-11, EurIPS 2025 workshop (preprint-level venue)] A 2025 follow-up formalizes the equivalence constructively: the MDM objective decomposes exactly into weighted autoregressive losses over decoding orders, with a direct mapping between decoding order and multivariate noise schedules (breaking objective invariance to the schedule); hence MDMs 'train to decode tokens in a random order' and the order itself can be optimized for performance (Garg, Kohli, Sarawagi; EurIPS 2025 PriGM workshop).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19152>
- [S|high|2024-12, ICLR 2025] Absorbing vs uniform is a real design fork: absorbing/[MASK] noise dominates language benchmarks (D3PM onward), but tokens are locked once revealed — no self-correction; uniform-noise diffusion (UDLM) can continuously edit outputs during denoising, benefits substantially more from classifier-based/classifier-free guidance (derived simply in this paper), and the absorbing-vs-uniform gap becomes negligible or reverses for small-vocabulary domains (genomics, molecules) — relevant for a math DLLM with a small arithmetic vocabulary (Schiff et al., ICLR 2025).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10193>
- [S|high|2025-03, ICML 2025] GIDD generalizes masked diffusion to any linear interpolation between data and a time-varying mixing distribution; hybrid mask+uniform noise training yields a novel ELBO, compute-matched SOTA diffusion-LM performance, better sample quality than mask-only models, and unlocks self-correction — the model can identify and fix its own already-generated tokens (Rutte et al., code github.com/dvruette/gidd).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04482>
- [A|high|2023-01-09] Why text diffusion lagged image diffusion (expert analysis, DeepMind's Sander Dieleman, Jan 2023): (1) Gaussian noise levels map to spatial-frequency feature scales that match human visual perception, but 'what does high frequency even mean' for discrete symbolic text; (2) discreteness forces either a discrete corruption process or embedding-space diffusion, each losing benefits; (3) AR training gets useful gradient signal from every position in one forward pass while diffusion samples one noise level per example, and AR sampling exploits KV caching — his 2023 verdict: 'still too early to consider diffusion a serious alternative to autoregression at scale.'  <https://sander.ai/2023/01/09/diffusion-language.html>
- [S|high|2023-05, NeurIPS 2023] Continuous embedding-space diffusion LMs pay a large, scale-invariant compute tax: Plaid's scaling-law analysis states verbatim 'Regardless of scale, Plaid models require a constant factor of about 64x more compute to match their autoregressive equivalents' (same scaling slope, constant offset). Plaid uses learned embeddings with CDCD-style categorical reparameterization; Plaid 1B outperforms GPT-2 124M in zero-shot likelihood (Gulrajani & Hashimoto, NeurIPS 2023). This 64x applies to continuous-relaxation diffusion, not masked discrete diffusion.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18619>
- [S|high|2024-10 (rev. 2025-02), ICLR 2025] For masked discrete diffusion the measured compute gap is ~16x, not 64x: the first MDM scaling law (SMDM, up to 1.1B params on SlimPajama) shows a scaling rate comparable to ARMs with 'a relatively small compute gap'; 'MDMs with 16 times more pre-training time offer a flexible trade-off against ARMs with KV-Cache: MDMs match ARMs in performance while being 1.4 times faster during sampling.' The same 1.1B MDM achieves math reasoning competitive with 7B Llama-2 on GSM8K, and the paper introduces unsupervised classifier-free guidance (Nie et al., ICLR 2025).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18514>
- [S|high|2025-02, ICML 2025] Feasibility-critical theory for a math-solving DLLM: under mild conditions MDMs achieve near-optimal perplexity with sampling steps independent of sequence length (efficiency win), but when measured by sequence error rate — required for whole-answer correctness on reasoning tasks — the number of sampling steps must scale linearly with sequence length, erasing the parallel-decoding efficiency advantage over AR; whether diffusion 'wins' depends on the evaluation metric (Feng et al.).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09622>
- [S|high|2025-02, ICML 2025] Existence proof at scale: LLaDA trains a masked diffusion model from scratch (forward random masking, reverse mask-prediction with a Transformer, cross-entropy bound on masked tokens) at 8B params / 2.3T tokens; LLaDA 8B Base surpasses LLaMA2 7B on nearly all of its evaluated tasks and is overall competitive with LLaMA3 8B, with SFT instruction-following — the masked-diffusion objective works at modern LLM scale (Nie, Zhu et al., Renmin U/ML-GSAI).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992>
- [S|high|2024-10, ICLR 2025] Mechanism-level advantage for math/planning: discrete diffusion's any-order, easy-first generation addresses 'subgoal imbalance' that AR models fail on — Multi-Granularity Diffusion Modeling reaches 91.5% on Countdown and 100% on Sudoku vs 45.8% and 20.7% for AR models without search (Ye et al., HKU NLP, ICLR 2025; code github.com/HKUNLP/diffusion-vs-ar) — direct evidence that the diffusion mechanism (not scale) can beat AR on equation-style search problems.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14157>
- [B|high|2025-10-20] Practitioner replication of the core mechanism is trivial at small scale: masked diffusion is 'a generalization of masked language modeling with variable masking rates' — a practitioner fine-tuned RoBERTa-base on WikiText-2 with a collator sampling mask probability 0.1–1.0 per batch, 30 minutes on one H200, then generated 256-token sequences by 10 iterative denoise/re-mask steps (100%→0% schedule, top-k/top-p per step); he cites D3PM's own statement that BERT is a one-step diffusion model. Confirms the from-scratch build path is BERT + variable masking + an unmasking schedule, not SDE machinery.  <https://nathan.rs/posts/roberta-diffusion/>
- [S|medium|2025-12-27, arXiv preprint (not yet peer-reviewed)] Open structural problems identified in a 2025 discreteness analysis: (i) uniform corruption does not respect how information is distributed across token positions, and (ii) token-wise marginal training cannot capture multi-token joint dependencies during parallel decoding — i.e., unmasking many tokens simultaneously samples from a product of marginals, not the joint; this is the theoretical root of quality loss at few sampling steps (Jin et al., preprint).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22630>
TENSIONS: SEDD's headline claim of beating GPT-2 on generative perplexity is partially undermined by arXiv 2409.02908: float32 Gumbel categorical sampling numerically lowers effective temperature, reducing diversity and making those generative-perplexity comparisons 'somewhat unfair'; zero-shot likelihood PPL wins stand, generation-quality wins are contested. || Compute-gap numbers conflict on the surface: Plaid reports ~64x (constant across scales) for continuous embedding-space diffusion (2023), while SMDM reports ~16x for masked discrete diffusion (2024), and MDLM shows only a ~10% perplexity gap at equal tokens (23.00 vs 20.86). Resolution: the gap depends on model family (continuous vs masked) and on metric (likelihood-match multiplier vs benchmark parity) — but every measurement agrees AR is more compute-efficient at equal budget. || Is it even diffusion? Three independent lines (RADD 2406.03736, time-agnostic 2409.02908, learned-order 2511.19152, seeded by ARDM 2021) prove masked diffusion training/sampling is equivalent to time-free any-order masked/AR modeling — contradicting the 'diffusion' framing SEDD/MDLM market. The equivalence is simultaneously exploited constructively (drop time-conditioning, cache outputs, first-hitting sampler), so the contradiction is about framing, not correctness. || Parallel-decoding speed promise vs correctness theory: MDM pitch is many-tokens-per-step, but 2502.09622 proves sequence error rate forces steps ~ O(sequence length), and 2512.22630 shows parallel unmasking samples from a product of marginals that cannot represent multi-token dependencies. Yet 2410.14157 shows diffusion decisively beats AR on Countdown/Sudoku — the win comes from flexible decoding order (easy-first), not from step-count efficiency. A math DLLM should expect order-flexibility gains but not free parallelism. || Absorbing vs uniform: absorbing/[MASK] noise wins language perplexity from D3PM through LLaDA, but vanilla absorbing locks tokens once revealed (no self-correction); UDLM shows uniform benefits more from guidance and matches/beats absorbing at small vocabularies; GIDD shows hybrid mask+uniform restores self-correction. There is no single 'correct' forward process — it is a task-dependent design fork, and small-vocab arithmetic is exactly the regime where uniform/hybrid is competitive.
GAPS: No published compute/scaling measurements at true laptop scale (<100M params, CPU or mobile GPU): all scaling-law evidence (Plaid 64x, SMDM 16x, MDLM, LLaDA) was measured at 85M–8B on cluster GPUs; the constant-factor gaps plausibly extrapolate downward but this is unverified inference. || D3PM's per-matrix-type quantitative text results (text8 bpc, LM1B PPL for uniform vs absorbing vs NN) were not extracted from the paper body — only the qualitative ranking (absorbing best for text) is verified here. || MD4's exact weighting function was not quoted from the MD4 paper itself (only 'weighted integral of cross-entropy losses'); the exact alpha'_t/(1−alpha_t) weight was verified from the MDLM project page, and the two papers' objectives are known to coincide, but I did not verify MD4's own notation. || arXiv 2512.22630 (discreteness analysis) and 2511.19152 (learned-order equivalence) are preprint/workshop-level and not yet validated by peer review or replication. || Expected but not found: a rigorous head-to-head of uniform vs absorbing diffusion on arithmetic/math token vocabularies specifically (UDLM's small-vocab evidence is genomics/molecules); whether hybrid GIDD-style self-correction measurably helps GSM8K-style answers remains an open question.

## DLLM model landscape & players — lens: Who / versus
- [S|high|2025-02-14 (v3 2025-10-18)] LLaDA 8B (GSAI/Renmin University + Ant Group collaborators) is the canonical open from-scratch masked diffusion LLM: trained on 2.3T tokens using 0.13M H800 GPU-hours, 4096-token context, vanilla multi-head attention + RoPE (no causal mask), SFT on 4.5M pairs. Objective: mask ratio t ~ Uniform[0,1], cross-entropy computed only on masked tokens with 1/t weighting. Math profile is its standout: Base GSM8K 70.3 / MATH 31.4 vs LLaMA3-8B Base 48.7/16.0 (diffusion base beats AR peer on math), while Instruct GSM8K 69.4 trails LLaMA3-8B-Instruct 78.3 (post-training gap). First posted 2025-02-14.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992>
- [S|high|2025-02 through 2026-06 (repo updates)] The official LLaDA repo is unusually honest about pitfalls: pretraining crashed once at 1.2T tokens and was resumed with LR cut from 4e-4 to 1e-4; the repo states current sampling is SLOWER than autoregressive baselines (fixed-length context, no KV cache, best quality needs sampling steps ≈ response length). Family timeline: LLaDA-8B Base/Instruct (Feb 2025, MIT license), LLaDA 1.5 with VRPO preference optimization (May 2025; GSM8K +4.7, HumanEval +3.0, later ACL 2026), LLaDA-V vision (May 2025), LLaDA-MoE-7B-A1B (Sep 2025, first MoE dLLM), iLLaDA (Jun 2026, Apache 2.0).  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA>
- [S|high|2025-08-21 (blog 2025-04)] Dream 7B (HKU NLP + Huawei Noah's Ark Lab; blog Apr 2025, paper Aug 2025) became the strongest open dLLM of mid-2025 by initializing from autoregressive Qwen2.5-7B weights (shift operation) plus context-adaptive token-level noise rescheduling, using only 580B tokens (~1/4 of LLaDA's data) from Dolma v1.7/OpenCoder/DCLM-Baseline. Base: GSM8K 77.2 / MATH 39.6 vs LLaDA 70.9/30.7. Planning tasks show diffusion's structural edge: Countdown 16.0 vs 6.2, Sudoku 81.0 vs 21.0 vs Qwen2.5-7B. But instruct-tier math still trails AR: Dream-Instruct MATH 39.2 vs Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 75.5, GSM8K 81.0 vs 91.6.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15487>
- [S|high|2025-06-17] Mercury (Inception Labs) is the first commercial-scale dLLM (announced Feb 2025; tech report Jun 2025). Mercury Coder Mini hits 1109 tok/s and Small 737 tok/s on NVIDIA H100, claimed up to 10x faster than speed-optimized frontier models at comparable quality; ranked 2nd on quality and fastest overall on Copilot Arena; throughput independently verified by Artificial Analysis. Architecture, parameter counts, and training data are NOT disclosed — it is a product report, not a reproducible recipe.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17298>
- [S|high|2025-11-06] Inception Labs is the commercial standard-bearer: founded 2024 in Palo Alto by Stefano Ermon (Stanford, co-inventor of diffusion techniques behind Midjourney/Sora), Aditya Grover (UCLA), and Volodymyr Kuleshov (Cornell); raised $50M (announced 2025-11-06) led by Menlo Ventures with Mayfield, Innovation Endeavors, NVentures (NVIDIA), M12 (Microsoft), Snowflake Ventures, and Databricks Investment participating — signaling infrastructure players hedging on the diffusion route.  <https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251106570339/en/Inception-Raises-$50M-to-Power-Diffusion-LLMs-Increasing-LLM-Speed-and-Efficiency-by-up-to-10X-and-Unlocking-Real-Time-Accessible-AI-Applications>
- [S|medium|2026-02-24] Mercury 2 (launched 2026-02-24) is billed as the first diffusion-based REASONING model and 'fastest reasoning LLM': >1,000 output tok/s (1,009 tok/s on NVIDIA Blackwell per coverage), end-to-end latency 1.7s vs 14.4s for Gemini 3 Flash and 23.4s for Claude Haiku 4.5 with reasoning enabled; priced $0.25/$0.75 per M input/output tokens via OpenAI-compatible API; Artificial Analysis publicly confirmed the >1,000 tok/s figure. Vendor-framed comparisons — treat quality parity claims cautiously.  <https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260224034496/en/Inception-Launches-Mercury-2-the-Fastest-Reasoning-LLM-5x-Faster-Than-Leading-Speed-Optimized-LLMs-with-Dramatically-Lower-Inference-Cost>
- [S|high|2025-05-20 (page current as of 2026-07)] Gemini Diffusion (Google DeepMind, unveiled at I/O 2025-05-20) claims 1,479 tok/s sampling (0.84s overhead) but was benchmarked only against Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite — a small speed-tier model, not frontier: HumanEval 89.6 vs 90.2, LiveCodeBench 30.9 vs 28.5, AIME 2025 23.3 vs 20.0. It remained an experimental waitlist demo and never shipped as a production API through mid-2026 — Google's own positioning is 'used to help develop and refine future models.'  <https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-diffusion/>
- [A|high|2025-05-21 and 2026-06-10] Independent expert verification of the speed story: Simon Willison measured Gemini Diffusion firsthand at 857 tok/s (May 2025), comparing the feel to Cerebras running Llama3.1-70B at ~2,000 tok/s; in June 2026 he ran DiffusionGemma via NVIDIA's NIM API at 500+ tok/s (2,409 tokens in 4.4s) and noted Google made 'no further announcements' about Gemini Diffusion in the 13 months between the demo and the open-weight release.  <https://simonwillison.net/2025/May/21/gemini-diffusion/>
- [S|high|2026-06-10] DiffusionGemma (released 2026-06-10) is Google's first open-weight text diffusion model: 26B Mixture-of-Experts with ~3.8B active params (HF id google/diffusiongemma-26B-A4B-it), Apache 2.0, weights on Hugging Face/Kaggle/Vertex AI; built on Gemma 4 + Gemini Diffusion research; generates 256 tokens per forward pass, >1,000 tok/s on a single H100, 'up to 4x faster' than comparable AR models. Google explicitly states it scores BELOW standard Gemma 4 on MMLU and coding and recommends Gemma 4 for quality-first production — an unusually candid quality-for-speed tradeoff admission.  <https://deepmind.google/models/gemma/diffusiongemma/>
- [S|high|2025-07-31] ByteDance's Seed team entered with Seed Diffusion Preview (2025-07-31; paper arXiv:2508.02193): an experimental code-focused dLLM hitting 2,146 tok/s inference — 5.4x faster than same-size AR models — while matching top AR models on code benchmarks and beating them on code editing. Techniques: two-stage diffusion training (mask + edit), constrained-order learning, and on-policy learning for parallel decoding. Stated goal: systematically validate discrete diffusion as the foundation framework for next-generation LMs. Parameter count undisclosed.  <https://seed.bytedance.com/en/blog/seed-research-seed-diffusion-preview-released-a-diffusion-language-model-delivering-breakthrough-2-146-tokens-s-inference-speed>
- [S|high|2025-09 to 2025-12] Ant Group (inclusionAI) is the scale leader in open dLLMs: LLaDA-MoE (arXiv:2509.24389, Sep 2025) trained a 7B-total/1.4B-active MoE dLLM from scratch on ~20T tokens, surpassing LLaDA/LLaDA 1.5/Dream; LLaDA2.0 (arXiv:2512.15745, Dec 2025) shipped LLaDA2.0-mini (16B) and LLaDA2.0-flash (100B MoE) — the first 100B-parameter diffusion LM — built by 'smoothly' converting an existing AR model to diffusion rather than training from scratch, with the flash-CAP variant reaching 535 tok/s via parallel decoding.  <https://github.com/inclusionAI/LLaDA2.X>
- [S|high|2025-10-09] Radical Numerics released RND1-Base (2025-10-09): a 30B sparse-MoE dLLM with 3B active params, converted from AR Qwen3-30B-A3B via 'simple, scalable AR-to-diffusion (A2D) conversion' with 500B tokens of continual pretraining — the largest open-weight base dLLM at release (weights, training details, and code all open). Scores: MMLU 57.2, GSM8K 72.1, MBPP 51.3. Together with Dream, RND1 and LLaDA2.0 establish AR-to-diffusion conversion (not from-scratch training) as the dominant cost-saving recipe of late 2025.  <https://www.radicalnumerics.ai/blog/rnd1>
- [S|high|2026-07-07] NVIDIA entered the model race with Nemotron-branded diffusion: 'Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion' (arXiv:2607.05722, submitted 2026-07-07; 26 authors incl. Song Han and Jan Kautz) is a tri-mode family (3B/8B/14B; base/instruct/VL) unifying autoregressive, diffusion, and self-speculation decoding in one architecture. The 8B decodes 6x more tokens per forward pass than Qwen3-8B at comparable accuracy and gets 4x throughput on a GB200 with SGLang; diffusion drafting + AR verification beats multi-token-prediction speculative decoding. Signals hybrid AR/diffusion convergence rather than pure-diffusion replacement.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05722>
- [S|high|2023-10 (paper) / 2024-07 (ICML award)] The academic lineage that makes laptop-scale builds feasible runs through SEDD (Lou, Meng, Ermon — ICML 2024 Best Paper, arXiv:2310.16834): score-entropy loss for discrete diffusion, 25-75% perplexity reduction vs earlier language diffusion, competitive with/beating GPT-2, and a quality-compute knob (similar quality with 32x fewer network evaluations). Open modular code plus small/medium pretrained checkpoints on Hugging Face make it a canonical GPT-2-scale reference implementation — the realistic size class for consumer-hardware training.  <https://github.com/louaaron/Score-Entropy-Discrete-Diffusion>
- [S|high|2025-03 (arXiv) / 2025-04 (ICLR)] Block Diffusion (BD3-LM, Cornell Kuleshov group — ICLR 2025 Oral, arXiv:2503.09573) is the key 'versus'-resolving paradigm: it decomposes sequences into blocks, runs discrete diffusion within each block, and autoregression across blocks — restoring KV caching and variable-length generation that full-sequence dLLMs lack, with block size tuning the AR-vs-diffusion tradeoff. Trained GPT-2-scale checkpoints (OpenWebText, block sizes 4/8/16) are on Hugging Face. This lineage (via SDAR) underlies the strongest later diffusion reasoning models.  <https://github.com/kuleshov-group/bd3lms>
- [S|high|2025-09-08 (paper) / ICLR 2026] For math specifically, the state of the art among open dLLMs is the TraceRL/TraDo line (Gen-Verse, arXiv:2509.06949, ICLR 2026): trajectory-aware RL applied to block diffusion models yields TraDo-4B/8B; TraDo-8B-Instruct posts +6.1% relative over Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and +51.3% relative over Llama3.1-8B-Instruct on math reasoning benchmarks (MATH500/AIME-class), and TraDo-8B-Thinking is the first 8B long-CoT diffusion model. The dLLM-RL GitHub framework ships SFT + RL recipes, KV-cache acceleration, and inference engines — the most directly reusable open stack for a math-specialized dLLM.  <https://github.com/Gen-Verse/dLLM-RL>
- [C|medium|2025-10] An inference-engine race is a proxy war among players: Ant Group open-sourced dInfer (Oct 2025), claiming ~1,011 tok/s average on HumanEval with LLaDA-MoE vs 91 tok/s for NVIDIA's Fast-dLLM and 294 tok/s for Qwen2.5-3B on vLLM — presented as the first open evidence of dLLM serving surpassing an AR baseline. The framework targets the three structural dLLM serving problems: high per-step compute, KV-cache invalidation under bidirectional attention, and quality loss from parallel decoding.  <https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3329138/ant-group-explores-ai-inference-framework-10-times-faster-nvidias-solution>
- [S|high|2025-07-21] The cleanest head-to-head science on 'diffusion vs AR' is CMU+Lambda's NeurIPS 2025 paper (arXiv:2507.15857): with fixed data reused over many epochs, masked diffusion models overtake AR models in validation loss and downstream performance (attributed to implicit data augmentation over token orderings/prediction tasks), but AR models win at low compute near the single-epoch Chinchilla-optimal point. Direct implication: at hobbyist compute budgets with unlimited fresh data AR is favored; diffusion's edge appears when you must repeat a limited dataset many times.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15857>

## Training objective & pretraining recipe for discrete diffusion language models — lens: How (what loss to implement, how to weight it, what schedule, what hyperparameters, what it costs, how to keep it stable)
- [S|high|2024-06-11 (NeurIPS 2024)] The modern canonical training objective (MDLM) is dead simple: a continuous-time NELBO that reduces to a weighted average of classical masked-LM cross-entropy losses, L = E_q ∫ (alpha'_t/(1-alpha_t)) * sum_l log<x_theta(z_t), x_l> dt, computed only on masked positions. It is derived via the SUBS parameterization: (1) zero masking probabilities — substitute the [MASK] logit with -infinity so the model never predicts MASK, and (2) carry-over unmasking — already-unmasked tokens are copied through (x_theta(z_t)=z_t at those positions). These two constraints Rao-Blackwellize the ELBO (drop stochastic terms), tightening the bound and improving perplexity.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07524>
- [S|high|2024-06-06 (NeurIPS 2024)] The continuous-time masked-diffusion ELBO is provably invariant to the functional form of the noise schedule alpha_t (change of variables gamma = log(1-alpha_t) in MDLM; MD4 shows only the endpoint SNR values matter). Practical consequence: for training the objective you can just use the log-linear/linear schedule alpha_t = 1-t (which makes the weight alpha'_t/(1-alpha_t) = -1/t, i.e. the familiar '1/t weighting'); schedule choice affects estimator variance and sampling behavior, not the loss in expectation.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04329>
- [S|high|2024-06-06] Although the ELBO is schedule-invariant in expectation, schedule DOES matter for sampling and variance: MD4 reports switching from linear to cosine schedule improved ImageNet 64x64 FID from 70 to 17 at T=256 steps (cosine slows unmasking early, reducing conflicting simultaneous unmasking); MD4 also generalizes to state-dependent (per-token-value) schedules (GenMD4). Schedules tested: linear alpha_t=1-t, geometric, cosine alpha_t=cos(pi*t/2).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04329>
- [S|high|2024 (repo, master branch as of 2026-07)] MDLM's exact verified default recipe (from the official repo config.yaml): Adam lr 3e-4 (beta1 0.9, beta2 0.999), weight_decay 0, global_batch_size 512, max_steps 1,000,000, gradient_clip_val 1.0, EMA 0.9999, precision bf16, noise: loglinear, diffusion: absorbing_state, parameterization: subs, time_conditioning: False, antithetic_sampling: True, sampling steps 128 with ddpm_cache predictor; sequence length 1024 (OpenWebText, GPT-2 tokenizer) or 128 (LM1B, bert-base-uncased tokenizer).  <https://github.com/kuleshov-group/mdlm>
- [S|high|2024-06-11] MDLM scale/results reference points: OpenWebText 1M steps ≈ 262B tokens gives test PPL <=23.21 vs AR transformer 17.54 and SEDD <=24.10 (a ~17% improvement in perplexity bound over SEDD at matched training); LM1B 1M steps ≈ 33B tokens (10M steps ≈ 327B) gives MDLM <=23.00 vs AR 20.86. The residual AR-vs-diffusion perplexity gap at matched tokens is visible and persistent.  <https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07524>
- [S|high|2024-06-11] Variance reduction over the timestep t is a built-in, load-bearing part of the recipe: MDLM implements a low-discrepancy sampler drawing correlated (antithetic/stratified) t samples across the batch instead of i.i.d. uniform, 'similar to Kingma et al. (2021)', to reduce ELBO variance — enabled by default (antithetic_sampling: True). Naive i.i.d. t-sampling with the 1/t weight is high variance.  <https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07524>
- [S|high|2025-02-14] LLaDA 8B is the only fully-documented from-scratch pretraining recipe at LLaMA scale: 2.3T tokens, 0.13M H800 GPU-hours, fixed sequence length 4096, per-sequence t ~ U[0,1] with each token independently masked with probability t, and 1% of sequences given random length U[1,4096] for variable-length robustness. LR schedule (Warmup-Stable-Decay): linear warmup 0 -> 4e-4 over first 2000 iterations, hold 4e-4 until 1.2T tokens, drop to 1e-4 for next 0.8T tokens, then linear decay 1e-4 -> 1e-5 over final 0.3T tokens. AdamW weight decay 0.1, global batch 1280 (local 44/GPU).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992>
- [S|high|2025 (repo)] LLaDA's official implementation of the loss (GUIDELINES.md) shows the exact code to copy: t = torch.rand(b); p_mask = (1-eps)*t + eps (eps avoids division blow-up at t->0); loss = F.cross_entropy(logits[masked], input_ids[masked]) / p_mask[masked] — i.e. the 1/t weighting is implemented as division by per-token masking probability. SFT variant: never noise the prompt, mask only response tokens, divide token loss by answer length, pad responses with multiple EOS tokens counted in answer length. Mask token id 126336, encoder Transformer with causal mask removed.  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA/blob/main/GUIDELINES.md>
- [S|high|2025-02-14] LLaDA SFT recipe and math capability proof point: SFT on 4.5M pairs, 3 epochs, batch 256 (local 22/GPU), LR warmup 50 iters to 2.5e-5, constant, then linear decay to 2.5e-6 in final 10%, weight decay 0.1. Resulting LLaDA 8B Base scores GSM8K 70.3 (4-shot) and MATH 31.4 — direct evidence that the plain masked-diffusion objective + SFT learns grade-school math at scale.  <https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09992>
- [S|high|2024-06-06 (ICLR 2025)] RADD proves the concrete score of absorbing-state diffusion factorizes as e^{-sigma_bar(t)}/(1-e^{-sigma_bar(t)}) * p_0(x_i | x_t^unmasked) — a time-dependent scalar times a time-independent conditional of clean data. Consequences: (1) the denoiser network needs NO time conditioning at all ('a form similar to the standard GPT model, with softmax as the final activation'); (2) four objectives — denoising score entropy (SEDD-style), t-DCE, lambda-DCE, and any-order autoregressive loss — are proven equivalent as total noise -> infinity (Theorem 2); (3) time-independence enables caching, with expected NFEs = n(1-(1-1/n)^l). Trained 400K iters on OpenWebText at GPT-2 scale, all RADD losses beat SEDD on 5 zero-shot benchmarks.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03736>
- [S|high|2024-09-04 (ICLR 2025)] Independent confirmation that time conditioning is unnecessary: 'Masked Diffusion Models are Secretly Time-Agnostic Masked Models' (ICLR 2025) shows both training and sampling of MDMs are theoretically free of the time variable (equivalent to order-agnostic masked models), proposes a first-hitting sampler with ~20x speedup, and exposes a numerical landmine: 32-bit Gumbel categorical sampling is inaccurate and silently lowers effective sampling temperature, making prior generative-perplexity comparisons (e.g., SEDD-era 'better than GPT-2' claims) partly an artifact. Use float64 for Gumbel sampling when evaluating.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02908>
- [S|high|2024-10-24 (ICLR 2025)] The honest compute-efficiency gap: in IsoFLOP scaling experiments (6e18 to 1e20 FLOPs, up to 1.1B params, SlimPajama 627B tokens, linear schedule alpha_t = 1-t, context 2048), masked diffusion models require approximately 16x more compute than autoregressive models to reach comparable validation loss — but the power-law scaling RATE matches AR, and MDMs win where AR structurally fails: 92% vs 0% (Llama-2 13B) on reverse-curse tasks and better temporal robustness (PPL 24.06 vs 27.01 on 2024 data).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18514>
- [S|high|2025-07-21] The counterweight to the 16x gap, and the most important fact for a laptop-scale build: in data-constrained settings (small unique corpus, many epochs), diffusion BEATS AR. Critical compute crossover C_crit(U) = 2.12x10^1.956 * U^2.174; data-reuse half-life RD* ≈ 512.85 epochs for diffusion vs 31.93 for AR — AR gains saturate around ~4 epochs of repetition while diffusion keeps matching the fresh-data curve up to ~100 epochs. Mechanism: random masking ratios/orders act as implicit data augmentation (AR trained with 16 random orderings approaches diffusion). Decision rule stated by authors: compute-constrained -> AR; data-constrained -> diffusion. Setup: 7M-2.5B params, C4, U in {25M, 50M, 100M, 500M} unique tokens, up to 800 epochs.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15857>
- [S|high|2024-10-23 (ICLR 2025)] The cheapest validated path to a capable diffusion LM is NOT from-scratch pretraining but adapting an existing AR checkpoint (DiffuGPT/DiffuLLaMA, ICLR 2025): keep the AR shift operation (position n-1 predicts token n), anneal the attention mask from causal to bidirectional (10K-step annealing for GPT-2; the 7B model skipped annealing), add no time embedding, and continually pretrain with the 1/t-weighted masked cross-entropy: L = (1/t) E[-sum delta(x_t^n, m) x_0^T log f_theta(x_t)]. Token budgets: GPT-2 (127M-355M) adapted with 30B FineWeb tokens; LLaMA-2-7B with 65B tokens (SlimPajama 70% + Starcoder 30%), bf16 + flash-attention-2. Discrete diffusion loss adapts better than continuous-diffusion loss; fine-tuning from adapted DiffuGPT-Small beats direct DD-loss fine-tuning of GPT-2 on GSM8K-symbolic (50.2% vs 45.4%).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17891>
- [S|high|2025-04-02] Dream 7B (strongest open diffusion LM of its generation) confirms the adaptation recipe at scale: initialized from Qwen2.5-7B weights, then 580B tokens of diffusion training. The authors state AR initialization 'significantly reduc[es] the tokens and computation required' vs from-scratch (validated in a LLaMA3.2-1B ablation where AR-init loss stays lower throughout). Learning rate is THE critical hyperparameter: too high 'can quickly wash away the left-to-right knowledge in the initial weights', too low hinders diffusion training; they report no unrecoverable loss spikes. They also introduce context-adaptive token-level noise rescheduling because with discrete masking the effective per-token noise level does not match the sequence-level t.  <https://hkunlp.github.io/blog/2025/dream/>
- [S|medium|2025-11 (~8 months old)] Training instability is a real, quantified problem with named fixes: MDM training variance decomposes into (A) mask-pattern noise, (B) mask-rate (t) noise, (C) data noise — AR suffers only (C). Proposed fixes: P-POTS (Pareto-optimal parametric t-sampling: sample harder mask rates more often with smaller updates) and MIRROR (complementary/antithetic mask pairs), plus stratified t-sampling and bin-wise EMA control variates. Measured effect on GSM8K SFT of an MDM: standard training lands 50.6-53.7% across runs; P-POTS+MIRROR lands 58.6-62.0%, with run-to-run variability reduced to near-AR levels. Also notes official MDM repos adopt EMA by default as a necessary stabilizer.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18159>
- [S|high|2025-03-12 (v3 2025-05-17)] Block diffusion (BD3-LMs, ICLR 2025 Oral) interpolates the two paradigms: autoregressive over blocks, masked diffusion within each block — recovering KV caching and arbitrary-length generation while keeping parallel in-block sampling. The paper explicitly identifies high gradient variance as the reason diffusion lags AR in likelihood, and derives data-driven 'clipped' noise schedules (narrower mask-rate intervals) that minimize this variance, achieving state-of-the-art perplexity among diffusion models. For a laptop build wanting streaming UI + KV cache, this is the objective variant to consider.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09573>
- [S|high|2023-10-25 (ICML 2024)] SEDD (ICML 2024 oral) is the historical predecessor objective — 'score entropy' learns data-distribution ratios p(y)/p(x) via a tractable denoising variant, cut perplexity 25-75% vs earlier diffusion LMs and beat GPT-2 zero-shot at matched size. But it is now superseded for implementation purposes: MDLM/RADD/MD4 show the simple time-independent masked cross-entropy form matches or beats it (MDLM ~17% better PPL bound; all four RADD losses beat SEDD) with far less machinery. Build advice: read SEDD for theory, implement MDLM/RADD-style masked CE.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16834>
- [A|high|2023-01-09 (historical relevance explicit)] The mechanistic explanation of the compute gap (expert analysis, pre-dating the measurements): AR training extracts learning signal at EVERY sequence position per forward-backward pass, while diffusion training gets signal only at masked positions at one sampled noise level per pass, making it 'significantly less statistically efficient'; AR additionally benefits from KV caching at inference while diffusion needs full forward passes per step. Written January 2023 by Sander Dieleman (DeepMind), it correctly predicted why diffusion LMs would struggle to displace AR — the 2024 16x measurement is the empirical confirmation.  <https://sander.ai/2023/01/09/diffusion-language.html>
- [B|medium|2025 (repos; exact dates unverified)] Tiny/minimal implementations demonstrate the recipe works at laptop scale: nanoLLaDA (~500 lines, full pipeline: data download, tokenizer, train, generate, inspired by nanochat); nathan-barry/tiny-diffusion (character-level masked diffusion on Tiny Shakespeare, 10,000 iterations in ~20 minutes on a single A100 — proportionally hours on a mid-range laptop GPU); gumran/language-diffusion (<80-line training script fine-tuning DistilBERT into a LLaDA-style masked diffusion model on TinyStories, following Nie et al.'s objective, with an untested single-GPU non-accelerate path). These validate that the masked-CE + random-t recipe trains stably at toy scale without datacenter tricks.  <https://github.com/Lukas-Xue/nanoLLaDA>
- [S|medium|2026-06-24] The recipe frontier keeps moving but the objective does not: iLLaDA (June 2026, same RUC/GSAI group) scales the SAME masked diffusion objective to 12T pretraining tokens with fully bidirectional attention plus a 25B-token instruction corpus for 12 epochs (note: many-epoch SFT, consistent with diffusion's data-reuse advantage), gaining +14.5 MATH and +16.5 HumanEval for the instruct model and claiming competitiveness with Qwen2.5 7B — evidence that from-scratch bidirectional diffusion training is a viable path and that recipe (data/epochs/annealing), not a new loss, is where gains come from.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25331>
TENSIONS: Compute efficiency: SMDM (2410.18514) measures MDMs needing ~16x more compute than AR for equal validation loss, while Prabhudesai et al. - [S|high|June 2024 (paper); repo maintained with issues closed through March 2026] kuleshov-group/mdlm is the canonical research-grade PyTorch reference for masked diffusion LMs (NeurIPS 2024 'Simple and Effective Masked Diffusion Language Models', arXiv 2406.07524). Apache-2.0, ~702 stars. Includes DiT, AR-transformer, and Mamba backbones; OpenWebText and LM1B pipelines; a 'ddpm_cache' sampler claimed ~3-4x faster than prior diffusion sampling plus 25-30x faster semi-autoregressive decoding; and in-repo baseline implementations of SEDD and D3PM. Code split: main.py, diffusion.py, noise_schedule.py, dataloader.py, models/, configs/ on PyTorch Lightning. README examples reference A5000 and RTX 3090 GPUs, i.e., prosumer hardware, with pretrained OpenWebText checkpoints on HuggingFace.  <https://github.com/kuleshov-group/mdlm>
- [S|high|2024 (paper); issue activity into January 2026] louaaron/Score-Entropy-Discrete-Diffusion is the official SEDD implementation (ICML 2024 Best Paper, arXiv 2310.16834), MIT license, ~739 stars. Cleanly modular (noise_lib.py schedules, graph_lib.py forward process, sampling.py, model/), with small and medium pretrained checkpoints on HuggingFace and both unconditional and prefix/suffix conditional sampling scripts. However, training was done on 8x80GB GPU nodes with DDP — far beyond laptop scale — so it serves as a reading/sampling reference, not a local training recipe.  <https://github.com/louaaron/Score-Entropy-Discrete-Diffusion>

A from-scratch builder copying SEDD's score-entropy loss should expect NaN/stability landmines that the simpler masked-CE (MDLM/LLaDA-style) objective avoids.  <https://github.com/louaaron/Score-Entropy-Discrete-Diffusion/issues>
- [S|high|February 2025 (paper); repo updated October 2025] ML-GSAI/LLaDA (arXiv 2502.09992, ~3.9k stars) is the flagship 8B masked-diffusion LLM repo but releases NO training framework or data — only inference (generate.py, get_log_likelihood.py, chat.py), a Gradio demo (app.py) with animated unmasking output, and lm-evaluation-harness evaluation (May 2025) plus batch inference (Oct 2025). - [S|high|2025] LLaDA's GUIDELINES.md is the single most concrete minimal-change training recipe found: (1) take any AR transformer codebase and simply remove the causal mask from self-attention; (2) reserve a mask token (id 126336 in LLaDA); (3) per sequence sample t~U[0,1] and mask each position with probability p_mask=(1-eps)t+eps with eps=0.001; (4) loss = cross-entropy on masked positions only, each token's CE divided by its p_mask, averaged over batch; (5) ~1% of pretraining data uses random sequence lengths from [1,4096]; (6) for SFT: never add noise to the prompt, mask only response tokens, and normalize loss by answer length (accounting for padding) instead of 1/p_mask. This is directly implementable pseudocode for a from-scratch build.  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA/blob/main/GUIDELINES.md>
- [S|high|ICLR 2025] ML-GSAI/SMDM (ICLR 2025 'Scaling up Masked Diffusion Models on Text', Apache-2.0, ~384 stars) is the open training codebase LLaDA points to: full Lightning-based pretraining scripts for ARMs, MDMs, and MDMs with stochastic sequence length, trained on SlimPajama-627B at 170M-1.1B params using 8-16 GPUs. It establishes the first MDM scaling law (rate comparable to AR with a small compute gap), and its 1.1B MDM beats 1.1B TinyLlama on several zero-shot benchmarks and is competitive with 7B Llama-2 on GSM8K when trained with augmented math data — the strongest existing evidence that masked diffusion + GSM8K-style math works, but at ~1B scale, not 10M scale.  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/SMDM>
- [S|high|November 2025 (v2 simplified ~1000 to ~400 lines)] nathan-barry/tiny-diffusion is the closest existing artifact to the user's target project: a 365-line, 10.7M-parameter character-level text diffusion model on Tiny Shakespeare (MIT, ~923 stars), built by modifying the nanochat GPT with exactly 5 changes: add a mask token to vocab, switch causal to bidirectional attention, train on unmasking instead of next-token prediction, compute loss only on masked tokens, and generate via confidence-based parallel decoding. The repo ships a 313-line GPT twin (~80% code overlap) for side-by-side comparison, plus visualize.py that renders step-by-step denoising. Training: 10,000 iters in ~20 minutes on an A100 — implying feasibility on a mid-range laptop GPU (hours) and plausibly overnight CPU runs at reduced size (this extrapolation is inference, not measured).  <https://github.com/nathan-barry/tiny-diffusion>
- [B|high|November 2025] The tiny-diffusion Show HN thread (Nov 2025) contains practitioner detail relevant to the UI goal: simon willison (simonw) ported the project's matplotlib diffusion-process demo to a curses terminal UI showing live denoising — proof the real-time unmasking visualization is a solved, small problem. The thread also surfaces the fixed-length limitation (model unmasks a predefined [MASK] region; variable-length output is handled imperfectly via confidence-aware parallel decoding), notes the ReLU-squared activation was inherited from the nanoGPT-speedrun lineage rather than chosen deliberately, and points to NVIDIA's Fast-dLLM for inference acceleration.  <https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45876742>
- [S|medium|2025-2026 (post-nanochat, exact date unverified)] Lukas-Xue/nanoLLaDA is a ~500-line minimal LLaDA-style masked diffusion pipeline (Apache-2.0, ~110 stars) covering the FULL lifecycle a builder needs: tokenizer training, distributed pretraining loop, iterative-unmasking generation, SFT, CORE-benchmark evaluation, and a tutorial.ipynb walkthrough. Its headline run is a 477M model trained on ~9.2B tokens of ClimbMix in ~55 hours on 4x NVIDIA L4 (23GB) — above laptop scale at that size, but the code scales down (configs from 20M) and is the best single 'read this end to end' reference for a from-scratch build.  <https://github.com/Lukas-Xue/nanoLLaDA>
- [S|medium|2024 (exact last update unverified)] cloneofsimo/d3pm is a ~400-LOC faithful PyTorch recreation of the original D3PM paper (Structured Denoising Diffusion Models in Discrete State-Spaces, 2021) with a self-contained runner and a CIFAR-10 DiT variant (~308 stars). Useful specifically for understanding the uniform-transition-matrix formulation and the discrete posterior math that the newer masked-only repos skip; the maintainer documents deliberate simplifications (no truncated logistic reparameterization, inverse-linear beta schedule).  <https://github.com/cloneofsimo/d3pm>
- [S|high|NeurIPS 2024] google-deepmind/md4 is the official JAX implementation of 'Simplified and Generalized Masked Diffusion for Discrete Data' (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2406.04329; Apache-2.0, ~161 stars), covering text (OpenWebText, FineWeb-EDU) and images (CIFAR-10, ImageNet-64) with one config-driven entry point. Documented setup is 8x A100 at batch 128 (or 8 TPU v5litepod chips at batch 32) — a clean reference for the continuous-time masked objective and its generalized (GenMD4) state-dependent masking, but JAX and multi-accelerator assumptions make it a reading reference rather than a laptop template.  <https://github.com/google-deepmind/md4>
- [S|high|ICLR 2025 (March 2025)] kuleshov-group/bd3lms (Block Diffusion / BD3-LMs, ICLR 2025 oral; Apache-2.0, ~1.0k stars) is explicitly 'built off of MDLM' and solves two documented weaknesses of vanilla masked diffusion that a builder will hit: fixed-length generation (BD3-LM generates arbitrary-length sequences as multiples of a block size) and no KV caching (sampling.kv_cache=true supported). Block size interpolates between AR (small blocks) and full diffusion (large blocks). HuggingFace checkpoints include BD3-LMs at block sizes 4/8/16 plus baseline MDLM and SEDD models, and the codebase inherits MDLM's Lightning structure.  <https://github.com/kuleshov-group/bd3lms>
- [S|high|February 2026 (paper); active 2026] ZHZisZZ/dllm ('dLLM: Simple Diffusion Language Modeling', arXiv 2602.22661; Apache-2.0, ~2.7k stars) is the current unifying open toolkit: HuggingFace transformers.Trainer-based pipelines for pretraining, SFT, and GRPO RL across LLaDA (incl. MoE), Dream, Tiny-A2D (AR-to-diffusion adaptation), BERT-Chat (turning an encoder BERT into a chat diffusion model — a notably cheap path to a small working DLM), and Edit Flows; LoRA/DeepSpeed/FSDP support; lm-evaluation-harness integration; Fast-dLLM cached inference; and an interactive chat.py. Documented examples assume 8-GPU nodes (evaluation ~4 GPUs), so laptop use means scaling configs down or LoRA.  <https://github.com/ZHZisZZ/dllm>
- [S|medium|2025 (exact release date unverified)] pengzhangzhi/Open-dLLM (Open-dCoder, 0.5B; Apache-2.0, ~645 stars) claims to be the first diffusion-LLM release opening the ENTIRE stack — pretraining code (VeOmni/torchrun), data pipeline (FineCode on HuggingFace), evaluation (HumanEval/MBPP/infilling via lm-eval-harness + custom), inference, and checkpoints — with a README comparison table showing LLaDA and Dream release only inference/weights. Initialization is continued pretraining from Qwen2.5-Coder. For a builder, it is the most complete end-to-end pretraining example at sub-1B scale, albeit for code rather than math.  <https://github.com/pengzhangzhi/Open-dLLM>
- [S|high|February 2025 (ICLR 2025)] HKUNLP/DiffuLLaMA (ICLR 2025 'Scaling Diffusion Language Models via Adaptation from Autoregressive Models'; Apache-2.0, ~401 stars) provides the cheapest documented route to a working diffusion LM: continual pretraining that converts existing AR checkpoints (GPT-2 ~127M/medium to DiffuGPT; LLaMA-7B to DiffuLLaMA) using attention-mask annealing (gradual causal-to-bidirectional transition) and a shift operation, in under 200B tokens total, with full training code, LoRA fine-tuning, and evaluation released (llamafactory-cli + DeepSpeed ZeRO). Adapting GPT-2-small this way is far closer to laptop-feasible than from-scratch pretraining at equal quality.  <https://github.com/HKUNLP/DiffuLLaMA>
- [S|high|April 2025 (release); August 2025 (paper)] DreamLM/Dream (Dream 7B, arXiv 2508.15487; Apache-2.0, ~1.3k stars) releases inference, evaluation, SFT code (single-node 8-GPU), Gradio demo, and a diffusion_generate() API whose remasking strategies ('origin', 'maskgit_plus', 'topk_margin', 'entropy') plus token/logits hooks are a ready-made menu of sampling algorithms to reimplement in a small model and to drive a denoising-visualization UI. Stated requirement of a 20GB+ VRAM GPU puts running Dream itself outside most consumer laptops.  <https://github.com/DreamLM/Dream>
- [S|high|NeurIPS 2024] HKUNLP/diffusion-of-thoughts (NeurIPS 2024 'Diffusion of Thoughts', ~213 stars) is the main existing codebase for the exact target task — math reasoning with diffusion LMs: it fine-tunes Plaid-1B and SEDD on GSM8K and 4x4/5x5 digit multiplication, with train.py, batch evaluation, DPM-Solver acceleration, and self-consistency decoding, demonstrating a compute-vs-reasoning-accuracy tradeoff and self-correction behavior. Evidence that GSM8K fine-tuning of a pretrained discrete diffusion checkpoint works, but at ~1B params (GPU class), not laptop-pretraining scale.  <https://github.com/HKUNLP/diffusion-of-thoughts>
- [S|high|May-July 2026 (paper); RADD ICLR 2025] Recent training-dynamics literature identifies concrete pitfalls a from-scratch builder inherits silently: 'Understanding and Accelerating the Training of Masked Diffusion Language Models' (arXiv 2605.13026, submitted May 13 2026, revised Jul 23 2026) attributes slow MDM training to locality bias under uniform timestep sampling and shows bell-shaped time sampling reaches equal validation performance ~4x faster on LM1B. Complementarily, ML-GSAI/RADD (ICLR 2025, ~84 stars, built on SEDD, 162M/405M checkpoints) proves time-conditioning of the network is UNNECESSARY for absorbing diffusion — removing it enables caching and unifies the objective with any-order AR — a major architecture simplification for a minimal build (matches MDLM issue #26 confusion about sigma vs t inputs).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13026>
- [B|high|April 2025 - March 2026] MDLM's issue tracker (all issues closed, #24-#36, 2025-2026) documents the practical friction points of reusing research repos on consumer machines: unpinned PyTorch versions (#24, #31), a 'no_flashattn' checkpoint that still required pip install flash_attn (#28) — flash-attn is notoriously painful on Windows/consumer GPUs — causal_conv1d_cuda import errors from the Mamba dependency (#25), a forward() keyword-argument bug (#27), and one paper-reproduction complaint that was resolved (#29). Implication for the build plan: strip optional CUDA-extension dependencies (flash-attn, Mamba) from any laptop-targeted reimplementation.  <https://github.com/kuleshov-group/mdlm/issues>
- [S|high|August 2025 (survey); maintained into 2026] VILA-Lab/Awesome-DLMs (~1.2k stars), the companion repo to 'A Survey on Diffusion Language Models' (arXiv 2508.10875), is an actively maintained (into 2026) index of DLM papers, codebases, training/inference frameworks (e.g., Fast-dLLM KV-cache acceleration, speculative/parallel decoding), benchmarks, and demos — the best single hub for discovering further reference implementations beyond those listed here. Also surfaced but not deeply verified: scxue/AO-GPT-MDM, a nanoGPT-based repo training any-order GPT as a masked diffusion model (lead for AR-architecture-reuse).  <https://github.com/VILA-Lab/Awesome-DLMs>

## Laptop-scale training feasibility for a from-scratch diffusion LLM — Economics / practitioner reality
- [S|high|record dated 2026-05-21; project since 2024-05] The modded-nanogpt speedrun trains a GPT-2-124M-class model to <=3.28 validation loss on FineWeb in 1.32 minutes on 8xH100 (record #84, May 21 2026), down from the 45-minute llm.c baseline of May 2024, using under ~400M tokens instead of 10B. The trick registry (Muon optimizer with Newton-Schulz, rotary embeddings, QK-norm, ReLU^2, FP8 matmuls, FlexAttention/FA3 with sliding windows, U-net/MUDD skip connections, value embeddings, batch/sequence-length scheduling, multi-token prediction) is the canonical list of software-side efficiency gains a laptop build can borrow — roughly a 30x token-efficiency and 30x wall-clock improvement over the naive stack at fixed hardware.  <https://github.com/KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt>
- [S|medium|2025 (repo; exact date not stated in README)] A single consumer RTX 4090 (24GB) can train GPT-2-124M quality from scratch in ~90 minutes: a port of the speedrun techniques (Muon, FlexAttention with document-causal masking, progressive context 256->1792, FP8 via torchao, value embeddings, U-net skips) reached 3.25 avg validation loss at step 1750 on ~0.92B FineWeb-Edu tokens at ~160k tokens/sec peak. This is the strongest direct evidence that modern efficiency tricks transfer to one consumer GPU; a mid-range laptop GPU is several times slower than a 4090 but the same recipe applies.  <https://github.com/Deveraux-Parker/nanoGPT_1GPU_SPEEDRUN>
- [A|high|2025-01 to 2025-03] An independent practitioner worklog (Jan 16 - Mar 3, 2025) reproduced the speedrun on 2x RTX 4090: baseline 8.13 hours to 3.28 val loss, reduced to 2.55 hours (3.2x) with Muon (largest single gain), RoPE, ReLU^2, trapezoidal LR, logit soft-capping (30), and FlexAttention; final run used 1.88B tokens vs 6.44B baseline. Confirms consumer-hardware replication of the efficiency stack by a second party, with times not comparable 1:1 to the 8xH100 leaderboard.  <https://www.tylerromero.com/posts/nanogpt-speedrun-worklog/>
- [S|high|2024-05-28] Karpathy's llm.c reproduced GPT-2 (124M) on 10B FineWeb tokens in ~90 minutes on one 8xA100 80GB node (~$14/hr on Lambda, ~$20 total, ~60% MFU), and he states you can train on a single GPU 'it would just take proportionally longer (e.g. ~4-24 hours depending on the GPU)'. This anchors the cloud-rental alternative: ~$20-75 buys what would be roughly a week of continuous laptop-GPU time for a 124M AR model on the pre-Muon stack.  <https://github.com/karpathy/llm.c/discussions/481>
- [S|high|2022-2023 (README)] nanoGPT's own baselines calibrate the CPU-only floor and the old-stack ceiling: char-level Shakespeare GPT trains in ~3 min on one A100 (val loss 1.4697); a shrunk config (context 64, batch 12, 4 layers, n_embd 128, 2000 iters) trains in ~3 minutes on CPU only to loss ~1.88; Apple Silicon MPS gives roughly 2-3x over CPU; and full GPT-2 124M reproduction on the unoptimized stack was ~4 days on 8xA100 40GB. CPU-only from-scratch training is therefore viable only for toy-scale (~1M-param, short-context) models.  <https://github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT>
- [A|high|2025-08-12] Direct laptop benchmark: on a 2024 MacBook Pro (M4, 24GB) using PyTorch MPS, the strongest model trainable in 5 minutes was a ~1.8M-parameter GPT-style transformer on ~20M TinyStories tokens reaching ~9.6 perplexity; a ~1M-param model sustained ~100k tokens/sec. Larger models did worse within the fixed budget (too slow per token), matching Chinchilla's tokens/20 sizing rule; torch.compile, float16 and gradient accumulation gave no benefit on MPS (GPU launch overhead was the bottleneck), and MLX gave no meaningful gain over MPS.  <https://www.seangoedecke.com/model-on-a-mbp/>
- [A|medium|2025-08-12] In the same laptop experiment, the author's discrete diffusion attempt (D3PM) 'produced only gibberish output' within the 5-minute budget while the equal-budget autoregressive model produced coherent story text. This is the only documented from-scratch diffusion-LM-on-a-laptop attempt found, and it indicates the diffusion objective converges visibly slower than AR at ultra-small compute — plan for hours (not minutes) of training and a masked (absorbing-state) objective rather than raw D3PM.  <https://www.seangoedecke.com/model-on-a-mbp/>
- [S|high|2023-05] TinyStories (Eldan & Li, Microsoft Research) shows models below 10M total parameters — even with a single transformer block — trained on a synthetic corpus of 3-4-year-old-vocabulary stories generate fluent, consistent multi-paragraph English with near-perfect grammar, while GPT-2 small (124M) trained on general web corpora 'can rarely generate coherent and consistent English text'. Domain restriction, not parameter count, is the lever that makes laptop-scale models look competent; the same logic applies to restricting to math.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759>
- [S|high|2023] Karpathy's llama2.c storyteller models (15M/42M/110M params on TinyStories) 'all trained in a few hours' on 4x A100 40GB, with the 110M taking ~24 hours (~96 A100-hours). Scaling down, a 10-15M-param model is a few tens of A100-hours, i.e. roughly days on a mid-range laptop GPU or a weekend on Apple Silicon — a realistic upper size band for a laptop-trained backbone.  <https://github.com/karpathy/llama2.c/blob/master/README.md>
- [A|high|2024 (original paper 2022-03)] Chinchilla (Hoffmann et al. 2022) established compute-optimal training at roughly 20 tokens per parameter (70B model / 1.4T tokens), scaling model and data equally with compute; Epoch AI's 2024 replication attempt broadly confirmed the compute-optimal ratio while critiquing the original parametric fit. Budget arithmetic for the build: a 10M-param model wants ~200M tokens, a 50M model ~1B tokens, a 100M model ~2B tokens — at a measured consumer-GPU rate of ~100-160k tok/s that is minutes-to-hours per epoch-equivalent, putting 10-50M AR models comfortably inside laptop budgets.  <https://epoch.ai/publications/chinchilla-scaling-a-replication-attempt>
- [S|high|2024-11-01] Competitive industrial small models are trained far beyond Chinchilla-optimal: SmolLM2-135M was pretrained on 2 trillion tokens (~740x the 20-tokens/param rule) and SmolLM2-360M on 4T tokens. A laptop-trained 100M-class model cannot approach this data budget, so expectations must be set against Chinchilla-optimal quality (GPT-2-ish), not against modern smol-model quality.  <https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-135M>
- [S|high|2024-10-24 (rev. 2025-02-28)] SMDM ('Scaling up Masked Diffusion Models on Text', ICLR 2025) establishes the first MDM scaling law with a scaling rate comparable to autoregressive models and reports that MDMs need up to 16x more pre-training time to match ARMs-with-KV-cache in generation quality (then sample ~1.4x faster); their 1.1B MDM beats same-data TinyLlama 1.1B on 4 of 8 zero-shot benchmarks and breaks the reverse curse. Their training used 8-16 data-center GPUs across 1e19-1.6e21 FLOPs — so for general text, budget a substantial 'diffusion tax' (up to ~16x) over the AR wall-clock numbers when planning laptop runs.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18514>
- [S|high|2025-07-21 (NeurIPS 2025)] 'Diffusion Beats Autoregressive in Data-Constrained Settings' (NeurIPS 2025) shows the diffusion tax inverts when unique data is limited and you train many epochs: masked diffusion achieves lower validation loss and better downstream performance than AR on repeated data, because randomized masking implicitly trains over many token orderings (implicit data augmentation); the paper derives a closed-form critical compute threshold where diffusion overtakes AR. A laptop math-specialist trained for many epochs on a fixed 10-100M-token synthetic corpus sits squarely in this diffusion-favorable regime.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15857>
- [S|high|2024-10 (ICLR 2025)] 'Beyond Autoregression' (ICLR 2025) trains discrete diffusion models at laptop-trainable sizes — configs of ~6M, ~85M, and ~303M parameters (LLaMA-Factory-based repo) — and finds Multi-Granularity Diffusion Modeling beats AR by huge margins on search/planning tasks adjacent to equation solving: 91.5% vs 45.8% on Countdown and 100% vs 20.7% on Sudoku, without external search. Direct evidence that small discrete diffusion models can outperform same-size AR models on structured symbolic tasks, supporting the math-specialization bet.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14157>
- [S|high|2023-12] TinyGSM shows small models solve GSM8K-style math when fed dense synthetic data: fine-tuning a 125M model on TinyGSM (12.3M GPT-3.5-generated grade-school problems paired with Python solutions) reaches 63.1% on GSM8K, and a 1.3B generator + 1.3B verifier reaches 81.5%. The dataset route (synthetic math problems + solutions) is the highest-leverage economics play for a laptop math model — data quality substitutes for parameters and compute.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09241>
- [S|high|2025-10] Karpathy's nanochat speedrun quantifies what $100 of rented frontier compute buys end-to-end: 3h51m on 8xH100 (~$24/hr, $92.40) trains a d20, 561M-param model on 11.2B tokens through SFT, scoring CORE 0.22 (slightly above GPT-2-large) but only 0.0455 on GSM8K — Karpathy notes it is 'not so sure ... how to do simple maths.' General-purpose pretraining at sub-$100 scale does not produce math ability; specialization (task-specific data, possibly tool use/verifiers) is mandatory for the math goal, and it also shows a laptop cannot compete with even $100 of cloud for general capability.  <https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat/discussions/1>
- [S|high|2025-02] Frontier-scale contrast for DLLMs: LLaDA 8B — the flagship open masked diffusion LLM — was pretrained from scratch on 2.3T tokens using 0.13 million H800 GPU-hours (order $200-400k of cloud compute), performing on par with LLaMA3 8B on many benchmarks. Competitive general-purpose diffusion LLMs are ~5 orders of magnitude beyond laptop training budgets; local from-scratch work must target 1M-100M params on narrow domains, or fine-tune released weights.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992>
- [S|medium|2024 (NeurIPS 2024)] MDLM (NeurIPS 2024, kuleshov-group) is the standard reference codebase for masked diffusion LMs: its OpenWebText model (~130M non-embedding params, GPT-2-medium-ish backbone, context 1024, 1M training steps) is on Hugging Face, and the paper's generation benchmarks were run on single consumer/prosumer GPUs (RTX 3090 / A5000) — consumer-GPU inference of a 100M-class masked diffusion model is proven, while the repo documents no laptop-scale training path (training was done at data-center scale).  <https://github.com/kuleshov-group/mdlm>
- [B|high|2024-04-09] Measured 8GB-laptop-GPU reality (RTX 4060 Laptop, fine-tuning Stable LM 2 1.6B, Apr 2024): on Windows the GPU silently spills past VRAM into shared system RAM instead of OOMing — just 0.9GB of overflow slowed training by ~45%, and a batch-size-2 run took 25.0 min/epoch vs 16.5 min/epoch at batch 1 (GPU power dropped 82W to 65W while waiting on memory); RTX 4060 thermal throttle point is 87C. For the build: a 5-50M-param model fits 8GB easily, but batch size/precision must be tuned to stay strictly inside VRAM, and sustained-load thermals are a real constraint on thin laptops.  <https://dev.to/maximsaplin/fine-tuning-llm-on-a-laptop-vram-shared-memory-gpu-load-performance-4agj>
- [S|medium|2022-01-06] Grokking (Power et al., OpenAI 2022) demonstrates that shallow transformers on small algorithmically generated datasets (modular arithmetic, p=97) reach perfect generalization long after overfitting; follow-up literature standardizes the setup as a ~2-layer, d_model=128, 4-head transformer on the order of 3x10^5 parameters. Pure arithmetic competence therefore needs well under 1M parameters — trivially CPU-trainable — but grokking-style training demands long optimization with weight decay, so wall-clock budget goes to steps, not size. Code: github.com/openai/grok.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02177>
TENSIONS: Diffusion compute tax vs diffusion advantage: SMDM (arXiv:2410.18514) reports MDMs need up to 16x more pre-training time than AR for equal generation quality, while arXiv:2507.15857 shows diffusion BEATS AR once you train many epochs on limited unique data, and arXiv:2410.14157 shows 6M-303M diffusion models crushing AR on Countdown/Sudoku. Resolution is regime-dependent: single-epoch, data-rich general text -> AR is far cheaper; multi-epoch, small fixed corpus (exactly the laptop math-specialist setting) -> diffusion is competitive or better. The build plan should exploit the second regime, not fight the first. || Practitioner failure vs paper success at small scale: Sean Goedecke's D3PM produced pure gibberish in a 5-minute MacBook budget while papers show small masked diffusion models working well — differences are objective (uniform-transition D3PM vs absorbing-state masked diffusion), budget (5 minutes vs full runs), and recipe maturity; this is a warning that the naive D3PM formulation is the wrong starting point, not that laptop DLLMs are impossible. || Parameter-count optimism vs data-budget pessimism: TinyStories says <10M params suffices for coherent text, but SmolLM2-135M needed 2T tokens to be a good general 135M model, and nanochat's $100/561M/11.2B-token model still scores only ~4.5% on GSM8K. Small-parameter success stories are always domain-restricted (stories, arithmetic, GSM8K-with-synthetic-data); a laptop model will only look good inside its niche. || 8GB VRAM 'insufficient' vs 8GB working in practice: aggregated buying-guide advice says 8GB is inadequate and 12GB+ is needed, yet the measured dev.to run fine-tuned a 1.6B model on an 8GB RTX 4060 Laptop successfully — the real failure mode is silent shared-memory spillover (-45% speed from 0.9GB overflow) rather than hard OOM, so careful batch/precision management makes 8GB fully workable for <=100M-param training. || Hardware headline vs software reality: identical GPT-2-124M quality cost 45 min on 8xH100 (llm.c, 2024) vs 1.32 min (modded-nanogpt, 2026) vs ~90 min on ONE RTX 4090 — a ~30x software-stack factor means published 'hardware requirements' for a given quality level are soft and mostly reflect the age of the training recipe.

## Sampling & inference algorithms for diffusion LLMs (how denoising/unmasking decoding actually works, how to accelerate it, and what it costs in quality)
- [S|high|2025 (repo active through 2025-2026)] The canonical LLaDA reference sampler (generate.py) works as follows: start from a fully masked response region; each step runs one full forward pass predicting all masked tokens; Gumbel-noise sampling implements temperature (logits.exp()/gumbel_noise, temperature=0 returns argmax); confidence = softmax probability of the sampled token; per step the top-k highest-confidence tokens are committed (k from get_num_transfer_tokens, which distributes mask_num evenly across steps — a linear commit schedule); remasking='low_confidence' keeps the rest masked; semi-autoregressive mode splits gen_length into blocks (num_blocks = gen_length // block_length) decoded left-to-right with full diffusion within each block; classifier-free guidance duplicates input with masked prompt and applies logits = un_logits + (cfg_scale + 1) * (logits - un_logits). Defaults: steps=128, gen_length=128, block_length=128, temperature=0, cfg_scale=0, mask_id=126336.  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA>
- [S|high|2025-02 (v2 later in 2025)] LLaDA achieves optimal quality when the number of sampling steps equals the response length (i.e., ~1 token committed per step on average); reducing steps degrades performance. The model runs with a fixed context length during sampling and recomputes full bidirectional attention every step, which is why vanilla dLLM inference is slower than autoregressive inference. Low-confidence remasking substantially improves over random remasking, and the instruct model works best with semi-autoregressive block decoding + low-confidence remasking. Accuracy consistently improves with more sampling steps, making steps the primary speed/quality knob.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992>
- [S|high|2025] Dream 7B exposes four unmasking-order strategies via its diffusion_generate() alg parameter: 'origin' (random order), 'maskgit_plus' (highest top-1 confidence first), 'topk_margin' (largest top1−top2 margin first), and 'entropy' (lowest-entropy token distributions first); alg_temp injects randomness into confidence-based orders; steps controls timesteps with max_new_tokens/steps tokens generated per step; temperature 0 is recommended for math/coding accuracy and higher temperature fixes repetition. This is the cleanest enumeration of practical unmasking heuristics to implement in a from-scratch sampler.  <https://github.com/HKUNLP/Dream>
- [S|high|2025-05 (v3 later)] Fast-dLLM (NVIDIA) proves a threshold theorem justifying confidence-aware parallel decoding: greedy parallel decoding of n tokens equals sequential greedy decoding when each token's marginal probability exceeds 1−ε with (n+1)ε ≤ 1. Practical recipe: decode all masked tokens whose confidence exceeds a threshold (default 0.9) in one step, plus block-wise approximate KV cache for bidirectional models — PrefixCache (cache prompt KV, refresh per block) and DualCache (also cache the masked suffix). Cache alone gives 2-3.6x, parallel decoding alone 4-6x.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22618>
- [S|high|2025] Fast-dLLM measured on LLaDA-8B, GSM8K (256-token generation), A100 80GB, batch size 1: baseline 6.7 tok/s at 79.3% accuracy; +KV cache 21.2 tok/s (79.5%); +confidence-parallel 16.5 tok/s (79.2%); both combined 54.4 tok/s at 78.5% (8.1x speedup, 0.8-point accuracy drop). Up to 27.6x end-to-end at generation length 1024 (8-shot). Cache block size 32 (optimal range 4-32). Repo (Apache 2.0) exposes --threshold, --block_size, --use_cache, --alg confidence_threshold for LLaDA and Dream, including a GSM8K eval path.  <https://github.com/NVlabs/Fast-dLLM>
- [S|high|2025-06; ICML 2026] dLLM-Cache (adaptive caching, ICML 2026): dLLM inference has a static prompt and mostly-stable response tokens across adjacent denoising steps, so it caches prompt features on a long interval (Kp ≈ 25-100 steps) and response features on a short interval (Kr ≈ 4-10), with 'V-verify' selecting only the ρ ≈ 0.25 fraction of response tokens whose value-vector cosine similarity changed most for recomputation. Measured on an RTX 4090: LLaDA-8B-Instruct GSM8K throughput 6.95 → 29.75 tok/s (4.28x) with accuracy 77.48% → 78.54%; Dream-7B GPQA 5.80 → 30.95 tok/s (5.33x); up to 9.1x FLOPs reduction on LongBench-HotpotQA; ~1GB (~5%) extra GPU memory.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06295>
- [S|high|2025] Vanilla dLLM decoding is roughly an order of magnitude slower than same-size autoregressive models on the same hardware: two independent papers measured LLaDA-8B baseline at 6.7 tok/s (A100, Fast-dLLM) and 6.95 tok/s (RTX 4090, dLLM-Cache), while a typical 8B AR model at Q4 reaches ~90-140 tok/s on an RTX 4090. Every denoising step is a full-sequence bidirectional forward pass with no KV cache, so total compute scales as steps × full-sequence attention. Conclusion for a laptop build: caching + adaptive parallel unmasking are mandatory design features of the sampler, not optional optimizations.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06295>
- [S|high|2025-10; ICLR 2026] ParallelBench (ICLR 2026; 17 tasks: 10 'Waiting Line' list-manipulation tasks, 5 text-writing tasks, 2 puzzles) shows parallel decoding quality collapse is governed by conditional total correlation C(Y|X): copy-like tasks (C=0) survive aggressive parallelism, shuffle-like tasks (unbounded C growing with length) degrade dramatically; the distributional error of T-step parallel decoding is lower-bounded by an information-theoretic term summing conditional total correlations. No decoding strategy was universally superior — random top-k sometimes beats confidence top-k on high-dependency tasks, and current adaptive (threshold) methods fail to adapt parallelism to task difficulty, leaving a large gap to oracle parallelism.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04767>
- [S|high|2025-10] For math specifically, aggressive parallelism is dangerous but adaptive entropy gating works: on LLaDA, fixed 2-tokens-per-step parallel decoding drops GSM8K accuracy from 76.95% to 62.31%, and full any-order entropy decoding scores 53.44% vs 75.96% for left-to-right; performant configurations behave nearly left-to-right anyway (leftmost position sampled ~50% of the time at block size 32). The paper's Multi-token Entropy Decoding (MED) — unmask extra positions only when their conditional entropy is below a threshold — recovers 2.7x speedup on GSM8K and 2.2x on HumanEval at baseline accuracy.  <https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19990>
- [S|medium|2026-01; ICML 2026] Decoding-order research converges on the same conclusion: learned/confidence-based unmasking orders trend left-to-right in practice, AR-like order achieves the best pass@k on GSM8K/MATH500 because solutions follow step-by-step structure, and 'The Flexibility Trap' (ICML 2026) argues arbitrary-order generation actively narrows the reasoning boundary because models exploit order freedom to dodge high-uncertainty tokens that are crucial for exploration. Build implication: a math-specialized dLLM should default to semi-AR small blocks with confidence ordering inside blocks, and the visualization should expect mostly near-left-to-right commit patterns on math problems.  <https://huggingface.co/papers/2601.15165>
- [S|high|2025-08] Prophet ('DLMs know the answer before decoding'): on GSM8K up to 97% and on MMLU up to 99% of instances can already be decoded correctly using only half the refinement steps; Prophet monitors the top-1 vs top-2 confidence gap and, when stable, commits all remaining tokens at once ('early commit decoding'), reducing decoding steps by up to 3.4x on LLaDA-8B and Dream-7B, training-free with negligible overhead. Reframes dLLM inference as a 'when to stop sampling' problem — a cheap, high-yield trick for a math-solver where final answers converge early.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19982>
- [S|high|2025-05; NeurIPS 2025] EB-Sampler (Meta FAIR, NeurIPS 2025): partially masked sequences often determine several unknown tokens near-deterministically, so a single forward pass carries unused information; the sampler adaptively unmasks a variable-size set of tokens per step whose cumulative entropy stays under a predefined error tolerance, acting as a drop-in replacement for standard MDM samplers and delivering ~2-3x acceleration on coding and math reasoning benchmarks without performance loss.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24857>
- [S|high|2025-03] Standard masked diffusion samplers can never revise a token once committed — an error made early is frozen. ReMDM derives a principled remasking backward process for pretrained MDMs that stochastically remasks already-decoded tokens, giving discrete diffusion true inference-time compute scaling: with more sampling steps output quality approaches autoregressive models, and under limited budgets it degrades more gracefully than vanilla samplers. Relevant as the 'self-correction' component the marketing story of diffusion LLMs usually implies but base samplers lack.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00307>
- [S|high|2022 (historical foundation, explicitly relevant)] MaskGIT (CVPR 2022) is the origin of confidence-based parallel unmasking: predict all tokens, keep the most confident, remask the rest, with the fraction kept governed by a cosine mask schedule γ = cos(π/2·τ) (commit few tokens early, many late); it completes generation in 8 iterations vs 256 sequential steps. The cosine schedule and max-softmax confidence selection are the direct ancestors of every dLLM decoder above, and a from-scratch implementation can start with exactly this loop.  <https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Chang_MaskGIT_Masked_Generative_Image_Transformer_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf>
- [S|high|2024 (still-canonical small-scale codebase)] For a from-scratch small-scale build, MDLM (NeurIPS 2024) ships the most reusable sampler code: its ddpm_cache sampler caches the forward output and skips recomputation on steps where no token changes, making sampling ~3-4x faster than SEDD/D3PM samplers (measured: 64 samples at T=5000 in 40.1s vs SEDD's 127.1s on a single A5000; T=10000 in 60.4s vs 229.3s), plus semi-autoregressive mode (sampling.semi_ar, stride_length, num_strides) for arbitrary-length generation that is 25-30x faster than SSD-LM. Config knobs: sampling.predictor ∈ {ddpm_cache, ddpm, analytic}, sampling.steps.  <https://github.com/kuleshov-group/mdlm>
- [S|high|2025 (Gemini/Mercury), dInfer 2025-10] Headline dLLM speed numbers come from datacenter hardware and custom stacks, not laptops: Mercury Coder Mini/Small hit 1109/737 tok/s on NVIDIA H100 (Inception Labs tech report); Google's Gemini Diffusion reports 1479 tok/s average sampling speed explicitly 'excluding overhead of 0.84 seconds', and its own eval table shows it losing to Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite on GPQA Diamond (40.4% vs 56.5%) while winning AIME 2025 (23.3% vs 20.0%). dInfer (inclusionAI) reaches 1100+ tok/s batch-1 for LLaDA-MoE but on 8×H800 GPUs, claiming 10x over Fast-dLLM. These validate the parallel-decoding ceiling but are not evidence of laptop-class speed.  <https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-diffusion/>
- [S|high|2026-05] NVIDIA Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion (2026) unifies three decoding modes in one open-weights model (3B/8B/14B): pure AR, diffusion ('fills in a 32-token block at a time by iteratively denoising, a confidence threshold decides which tokens commit each step', 2.6x tokens-per-forward), and self-speculation (diffusion drafts a block bidirectionally, AR pathway verifies causally, longest matching prefix commits — 6-6.4x tokens-per-forward, ~865 tok/s on B200, 'lossless versus AR at temperature 0'). Secondary reporting adds ~112 tok/s vs 41.8 AR baseline (2.7x) on a DGX Spark consumer-class box. Self-speculation is the strongest quality-safe parallel decoding pattern surfaced, and is implementable at small scale.  <https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/nemotron-labs-diffusion>
- [B|medium|2026 (recent commits)] CPU-only dLLM inference exists and is task-dependent in exactly the way that hurts a math specialist: the diffuse-cpp GGUF port of LLaDA-8B-Instruct (Q4_K_M ~5.1GB, Q8_0 ~8.4GB, F16 ~14.9GB) reports 27.7 tok/s on a 12-core AMD EPYC CPU for translation-style prompts (3.3x llama.cpp's 8.51 tok/s) using only 2-5 denoising steps, an inter-step cache worth 1.6x, and an 'entropy_exit' remasking mode — but complex reasoning/arithmetic prompts need 15-16 steps and run at ~0.6x llama.cpp speed, i.e. slower than an AR model on CPU. Math is the worst case for step-count reduction.  <https://huggingface.co/diffuse-cpp/LLaDA-8B-Instruct-GGUF>
- [A|high|2025] Sebastian Raschka's practitioner analysis of text diffusion models flags the deployment realities a builder must plan around: the steps knob is a raw speed/quality dial ('a low number of diffusion steps... may produce an answer with degraded quality'); parallel unmasking can produce incoherent combinations (the 'New' + 'City' failure mode) because tokens in one step are sampled independently; no token-streaming UX (output appears as a refining block — which is exactly what the requested denoising-visualization UI should render instead of a stream); and open questions remain on tool calling and chain-of-thought compatibility.  <https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/beyond-standard-llms>
- [S|medium|2026-04] Step-count saturation is consistently observed across the masked-diffusion literature: quality gains from more denoising steps are largest going from 1-2 to 4-8 steps, and task-average performance goes nearly flat around 16-32 steps in model-scheduling studies — meaning a small local model can budget steps per difficulty (few for easy arithmetic, up to steps≈length for multi-step word problems) rather than always paying steps=length. This is corroborated by Prophet's early-convergence measurements but the specific 8-32-step numbers come from search-level summaries of newer (2026) papers not fully fetched, so treat exact thresholds as indicative.  <https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02340v2>

## Math & reasoning specialization for diffusion LLMs — evidence and implications for a laptop-scale, math-specialized DLLM build
- [S|high|2025-02 (v1)] LLaDA 8B (first competitive from-scratch masked diffusion LLM) reports GSM8K 70.3 / MATH 31.4 for Base and GSM8K 69.4 / MATH 31.9 for Instruct in its own eval tables, beating LLaMA3 8B Base (48.7/16.0 as reported in the same tables); the authors explicitly flag math as a relative strength and attribute it partly to training-data composition, not architecture alone.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992>
- [S|high|2025-02] LLaDA 8B pre-training cost was 2.3T tokens and ~0.13 million H800 GPU-hours plus SFT on 4.5M pairs — establishing that LLaDA-class math scores (~70 GSM8K) are 5-6 orders of magnitude beyond laptop training budgets; a laptop build must target a different point on the curve entirely.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992>
- [S|high|2025-02] LLaDA 8B Instruct beats GPT-4o on reversal poem completion (45.6% vs 34.3%; forward: 51.8% vs 82.7%), showing masked diffusion's bidirectional training yields balanced forward/backward performance where AR models collapse — the flagship 'reversal curse' evidence for diffusion.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992>
- [S|high|2024-02 (v3 2024-12, NeurIPS 2024)] Diffusion-of-Thought (NeurIPS 2024): a tiny from-scratch diffusion model (DoT-scratch) hits 100% on 4x4 and 5x5 multi-digit multiplication at ~62 it/s vs GPT-2 CoT fine-tuned at 0.8 it/s — accuracy parity with >27x throughput — direct proof that small, cheap diffusion models can master structured arithmetic, the most laptop-feasible demo in the literature.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07754>
- [S|high|2024-02] DoT on GSM8K is a reality check: fine-tuned Plaid 1B reaches 32.6% (36.3% with self-consistency; multi-pass DoT-MP 37.7% → 40.3% with SC) vs GPT-2-small CoT fine-tuned 40.7% and ChatGPT 62.5% — natural-language math word problems do NOT automatically favor diffusion at ~1B scale; authors cite immature diffusion pretraining and need for task-specific fine-tuning as limitations.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07754>
- [S|high|2024-10] Beyond Autoregression (MGDM): from-scratch masked diffusion models at 6M/85M/303M params crush AR on search/constraint math tasks — Countdown-4: 91.5% vs 45.8% (85M GPT-2), Countdown-5: 46.6% vs 5.1%, Sudoku: 100% vs 20.7% AR (fine-tuned LLaMA-13B only 32.9%), Game-of-24: 76.0% vs 18.8%. Mechanism: 'subgoal imbalance' — left-to-right AR must emit hard tokens that depend on future context first; MGDM adds token-level loss reweighting v(x_t,n)=α(1−exp(−u))^β emphasizing hard tokens. Trained on ~500k generated Countdown problems. This is the strongest evidence that a laptop-trainable (<100M) diffusion model can visibly beat AR on math-adjacent tasks.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14157>
- [S|high|2025-04 (v2 2025-06)] d1 (Table 1, extracted from PDF): post-training LLaDA-8B-Instruct with SFT (s1K, 1000 examples, 20 epochs) + diffu-GRPO RL lifts GSM8K 78.2→82.1, MATH500 36.2→40.2, Countdown 20.7→42.2, Sudoku 11.7→22.1 (best sequence length per task); diffu-GRPO alone (81.9 GSM8K) beats SFT alone (81.1); gains from generation length 128→256 (~7.1%) exceed 256→512 (~2.5%). RL rollouts were capped at length 256 due to compute cost.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12216>
- [S|high|2025-04] d1 hardware truth (appendix, extracted from PDF): diffu-GRPO ran on 8x NVIDIA A100-80GB with LoRA r=128, batch 6/GPU; SFT used LoRA on 2x A6000, seq len 4096. The RL uses a one-step mean-field log-probability estimator (per-token independence) plus random prompt masking as regularization. Implication: RL post-training of 8B dLLMs is far outside laptop scope; LoRA-SFT of an 8B dLLM sits at workstation scale, and only <150M from-scratch training is genuinely laptop-plausible.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12216>
- [S|high|2024-10] SMDM (ICLR 2025) established the first MDM scaling law: masked diffusion needs ~16x more compute than AR to reach the same validation loss (models 220M-1.1B, 6e18-1e20 FLOPs, SlimPajama 627B tokens), but the scaling *rate* is comparable; the 1.1B MDM beats TinyLlama 1.1B (same data) on 4/8 zero-shot benchmarks and the abstract claims math reasoning on GSM8K competitive with Llama-2 7B.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18514>
- [S|high|2024-10] SMDM reversal experiment: a 1.1B MDM scores 92% on reverse queries (Berglund et al. fictitious name-description dataset) vs 0% for Llama-2 13B and GPT-3 175B, using ~10% of Llama-2's parameters/data and ~1% of its compute — quantitatively the strongest 'small diffusion beats giant AR on bidirectional recall' result.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18514>
- [S|high|2025-07] Diffusion beats AR in data-constrained settings (NeurIPS 2025): with repeated data, AR models extract value for only ~4 epochs while diffusion keeps improving up to ~100 epochs (best loss at ~500 epochs vs ~50 for AR), because random masking exposes diverse token orderings/prediction tasks; decision rule stated by authors: compute-constrained → AR, data-constrained → diffusion. Follow-up (arXiv 2510.04071) attributes most of the effect to random masking acting as stochastic regularization. Directly relevant: a laptop math build has a tiny unique corpus (GSM8K train = ~7.5k problems) that must be re-epoched heavily — the regime where diffusion wins.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15857>
- [S|high|2025-08 (blog 2025-04)] Dream 7B (AR-initialized from Qwen2.5 with ~1/4 of LLaDA's training data) reports GSM8K 77.2 (vs LLaDA 70.9) and much stronger planning: Countdown 16.0 and Sudoku 81.0 vs Qwen2.5-7B's 6.2 and 21.0, occasionally matching DeepSeek V3 on planning despite orders of magnitude fewer parameters — evidence that (a) diffusion's edge concentrates in constraint/planning tasks and (b) AR-initialization is a cheap route to a capable dLLM.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15487>
- [S|high|2025-10] 'Beyond Surface Reasoning' identifies the Parallel-Sequential Contradiction (PSC): dLLMs are genuinely parallel only for directly decidable outputs; as math/reasoning difficulty rises they revert to autoregressive-like sequential decoding, and autoregressive-style prompting nearly doubles decoding steps via remasking without quality gains. PSC restricts self-reflection, reasoning depth, and exploratory breadth; suggested mitigations are parallel-oriented prompting, diffusion early stopping, and parallel (best-of-n) scaling. This is the core contradiction to 'diffusion = better reasoner' claims for GSM8K-style sequential chain-of-thought.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09544>
- [S|high|2026-02] Fixed-length canvas is a real, documented handicap for math CoT: masked dLLMs must pre-commit generation length (too short truncates solutions; too long wastes compute and can degrade quality); d1's appendix notes LLaDA must take loss on PAD/EOS tokens to learn to terminate, and RL was locked to length 256. A 2026 line of work (length regularization arXiv 2602.07546; training-free bidirectional EOS control ρ-EOS arXiv 2601.22527; iLLaDA arXiv 2606.25331; Block Diffusion) exists specifically to fix variable-length generation — a laptop build should either use block/semi-AR decoding or fixed short canvases per task.  <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.07546>
- [S|high|2023-12] TinyGSM (AR-side calibration): a 1.3B generator + 1.3B verifier fine-tuned on 12.3M GPT-3.5-synthesized GSM8K-style problems with Python solutions reaches 81.5% GSM8K, beating its GPT-3.5 teacher (77.4%) — the decisive lever at small scale is high-quality synthetic data plus verification, not parameter count; the TinyGSM corpus (11.8M problems) is public and reusable for a diffusion math model.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09241>
- [S|high|2026-05] A concrete laptop-adjacent recipe exists (extracted from PDF): 2026 work trains a 125M masked diffusion model (Qwen2-style attention, hidden 512, 14 layers, 8 heads, seq len 512) on TinyGSM (11.8M problems) and evaluates GSM8K with Top-2/Top-3 confidence unmasking; LoopMDM (looping 2 early-middle layers, Smax=8) adds up to +8.5 GSM8K points over the same-size MDM and matches its accuracy with 43% of training FLOPs, with accuracy improving monotonically as inference-time loop count rises (test-time compute scaling knob). Baseline setup: 900k iterations, batch 32/GPU on 4 A100s, AdamW lr 3e-4, EMA 0.9999 — the architecture is exactly laptop-class even though the reference training run is not; the underlying protocol comes from PUMA (arXiv 2602.10314), which reports ~2.5x pretraining speedup at 125M scale and open-sources its codebase.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26106>
- [S|high|2025-05 (announced; page accessed 2026-07)] Industry frontier data point: Google's Gemini Diffusion beats its size-matched AR sibling Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite on math AIME 2025 (23.3% vs 20.0%) and MBPP (76.0 vs 75.8) while losing on GPQA Diamond (40.4 vs 56.5), Global MMLU (69.1 vs 79.0) and BIG-Bench Extra Hard (15.0 vs 21.0), at 1479 tokens/sec — the win pattern (math/code yes, broad knowledge/reasoning no) matches the academic evidence that iterative refinement helps math specifically.  <https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-diffusion/>

## Architecture internals — lens: How (DLLM backbone design, time conditioning, mask-token mechanics, BERT relationship, tokenizer/vocab choices for math)
- [S|high|2025-02-14 (v3 2025-10-18)] LLaDA (the flagship open masked-diffusion LLM) is architecturally a completely standard Transformer whose only structural change vs an autoregressive LLM is removing the causal attention mask: a 'mask predictor' Transformer takes a partially masked sequence and predicts all masked tokens simultaneously with full bidirectional attention, trained by 'a forward data masking process and a reverse generation process, parameterized by a Transformer to predict masked tokens' optimizing a likelihood lower bound.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992>
- [S|high|2025 (v3 2025-10-18)] LLaDA deliberately uses vanilla multi-head attention instead of grouped-query attention because bidirectional diffusion breaks KV caching: 'We use vanilla multi-head attention instead of grouped query attention for simplicity, as LLaDA is incompatible with KV caching, resulting in a different number of key and value heads.' Design implication for a from-scratch build: GQA/MQA buys nothing in a pure (non-block) masked diffusion model.  <https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09992v3>
- [S|high|2025 (model released Feb 2025)] Exact LLaDA-8B architecture from the official HuggingFace config.json: d_model 4096, n_layers 32, n_heads 32, n_kv_heads 32 (i.e., no GQA), mlp_hidden_size 12288, activation silu (SwiGLU-family), layer_norm_type rms (eps 1e-5), RoPE with theta 500000, max_sequence_length 4096, block_type 'llama', vocab_size 126464, mask_token_id 126336, eos/pad 126081, flash_attention false. This is a directly copyable reference for a scaled-down clone.  <https://huggingface.co/GSAI-ML/LLaDA-8B-Base/resolve/main/config.json>
- [S|high|2025] The official LLaDA training guidelines show the entire from-scratch recipe is a few lines on top of any AR codebase: sample t~U[0,1], p_mask=(1-eps)*t+eps, replace sampled positions with reserved mask id 126336 (noisy_batch = torch.where(masked_indices, 126336, input_ids)), compute cross-entropy only on masked positions divided by p_mask (the 1/t ELBO weighting), remove the causal mask ('simply removing the causal mask from the self-attention mechanism'); plus a robustness trick: 'We set 1% of the pre-training data to a random length uniformly sampled from [1, 4096]'; for SFT, prompts are never noised and loss is normalized by answer length.  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA/blob/main/GUIDELINES.md>
- [S|high|2024-06 (ICLR 2025)] Time conditioning is provably unnecessary for masked (absorbing-state) diffusion: RADD (ICLR 2025) shows the concrete score decomposes into time-independent conditional probabilities of clean data times an analytic time-dependent scalar, so 'a dedicated diffusion model without time-condition' matches or beats time-conditioned models, and the time-independent network output can be cached across sampling steps whenever no token changes, cutting NFEs. The LLaDA repo states this is 'the theoretical justification for LLaDA's unmodified use of the Transformer architecture.'  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03736>
- [S|high|2024-06 (NeurIPS 2024)] MDLM empirically confirmed the same thing before the proof: 'On OWT, we train with and without time step conditioning' and 'models trained with and without time conditioning attain similar perplexities on OWT' — so a from-scratch build can skip time embeddings and adaLN modulation entirely and use a plain pre-LN encoder-style transformer.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07524>
- [S|high|2024-06] MDLM's backbone lineage, if you do want the DiT-style option: 'We utilize the transformer architecture from Lou et al. (2023) [SEDD], which augments the diffusion transformer (Peebles & Xie, 2023) with rotary embeddings (Su et al., 2021)' — i.e., DDiT = encoder transformer + RoPE + (optional) adaLN time conditioning; experiments used 110M params, GPT-2 tokenizer for OpenWebText and bert-base-uncased tokenizer for LM1B, context length 1024.  <https://s-sahoo.com/mdlm/>
- [S|high|2024-06] MDLM's SUBS parameterization gives two implementation-level output-head rules that simplify the loss to weighted masked cross-entropy: (1) Zero Masking Probabilities — 'we substitute the logit index corresponding to the [MASK] token with −∞' so the network can never predict MASK; (2) Carry-Over Unmasking — the network output is substituted 'to simply copy unmasked inputs', so already-revealed tokens are passed through unchanged. With these, the continuous-time NELBO becomes a Rao-Blackwellized mixture of classical MLM losses.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07524>
- [S|high|2024-09 (ICLR 2025)] A second ICLR 2025 paper proves the equivalence from the sampling side too: 'both training and sampling of MDMs are theoretically free from the time variable... and are instead equivalent to masked models'; its first-hitting sampler (FHS) is theoretically equivalent to the original MDM generation process with a ~20x speedup; it also warns that reported MDM generative-perplexity wins partly 'exploit inaccurate categorical sampling' under low floating-point precision (a Gumbel-sampling numerical pitfall to avoid when evaluating your own model).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02908>
- [A|high|2026] The BERT relationship, stated by the lab that built MDLM: masked diffusion is 'essentially BERT with a randomized masking rate — but unlike BERT, the resulting model is generative. You can think of masked diffusion as a generative BERT.' The ELBO's inner term 'is exactly the BERT loss', differing only in averaging over all masking rates t~U[0,1] with 1/t normalization instead of BERT's fixed ~15% rate. The post also catalogs production architecture variants: encoder + lightweight iterative decoder (Gemma Diffusion, Nemotron Diffusion) and block diffusion, which restores KV caching by diffusing block-by-block conditioned on previously generated tokens.  <https://kuleshov-group.github.io/blog/blog/2026/how-to-build-a-diffusion-language-model/>
- [S|high|2023-07 (ACL 2023)] BERT weights can directly initialize a discrete diffusion LM: DiffusionBERT (ACL 2023) showed 'pre-trained denoising language models (e.g., BERT) can be used as a good initialization that accelerates convergence' for the reverse process, investigated several designs for incorporating the time step into BERT, and proposed a token-frequency-aware ('spindle') noise schedule — significant improvement over D3PM and Diffusion-LM on perplexity/BLEU.  <https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.248/>
- [S|high|2024-10 (ICLR 2025)] You can convert an existing AR checkpoint instead of training from scratch: DiffuGPT/DiffuLLaMA (ICLR 2025) adapt GPT-2 (127M/355M) and LLaMA-7B into masked diffusion LMs via continual pretraining using 'less than 200B tokens', exploiting connections between AR and diffusion objectives; resulting models outperform earlier diffusion LMs and can infill without prompt reordering. For a laptop build, adapting a small pretrained GPT-2 is a legitimate shortcut path.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17891>
- [A|high|2025 (paper 2025-08)] Dream 7B confirms AR-init at scale and documents the key alignment trick: it was initialized from Qwen2.5 7B weights ('using the weights from the existing autoregressive (AR) model serves as a non-trivial initialization... more effective than training from scratch'), and 'the model predicts all the masked tokens in a shifted manner, allowing for maximally architectural alignment and weight initialization with AR models' (shifted prediction keeps the AR output-head weight alignment). It adds context-adaptive token-level noise rescheduling that 'dynamically reassigns the noise level for each token based on the corrupted context'; trained on 580B tokens with fully bidirectional attention.  <https://hkunlp.github.io/blog/2025/dream/>
- [S|high|2024-10 (v3 2025-02)] From-scratch masked diffusion scales predictably and does math: SMDM established 'the first scaling law for MDMs, demonstrating a scaling rate comparable to autoregressive models' with a relatively small compute gap; a 1.1B MDM achieved 'competitive math reasoning ability with the 7B Llama-2 model on the GSM8K dataset' and breaks the reversal curse that afflicts much larger AR models. The LLaDA authors explicitly point people to SMDM's open-sourced training framework for training their own model — directly relevant reference code for a math-specialized DLLM.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18514>
- [S|high|2022-12 (ICCV 2023)] Context on the conditioning mechanism text models inherited then discarded: the original image DiT (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) conditions on timestep via adaLN-Zero (zero-initialized adaptive LayerNorm modulation), which outperformed cross-attention and in-context conditioning while adding negligible FLOPs and drove SOTA ImageNet FID 2.27. Text masked-diffusion work (SEDD→MDLM→RADD/LLaDA) progressively showed this whole apparatus is droppable for absorbing-state text diffusion — the number of masked tokens already encodes the noise level.  <https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Peebles_Scalable_Diffusion_Models_with_Transformers_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf>
- [S|high|2024-02] Number tokenization measurably decides arithmetic ability: 'Tokenization counts' (Singh & Strouse, DeepMind) found LLaMA/PaLM use single-digit tokenization while GPT-3.5/4 use 1-3 digit tokens; forcing right-to-left grouping (via commas) 'leads to largely improved performance' on arithmetic, L2R errors follow stereotyped patterns, and the R2L-vs-L2R gap shrinks with model scale — implying tokenization choice matters MOST at the small scales of a laptop build.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14903>
- [A|high|2024-07-07] Practitioner census and magnitudes for digit tokenization (Max Buckley, guest post on Beren's blog): single-digit tokenizers — Mistral, Llama 1-2, Gemma; L2R 3-digit chunks — GPT-3.5/4/4o, Gemini 1.5, Llama 3; R2L 3-digit — Claude 3+. Forcing R2L on 7-9 digit addition raised GPT-3.5 from 50.3% to 97% and Claude 2.1 from 28.3% to 100%. For a small math-specialized model, single-digit tokenization is the simplest scheme with the best small-scale evidence; R2L 3-digit is the efficient alternative.  <https://www.beren.io/2024-07-07-Right-to-Left-Integer-Tokenization/>
- [S|high|2024-07 (NeurIPS 2024)] Vocabulary size should shrink with model size: NeurIPS 2024 vocabulary scaling laws find optimal vocab parameters scale as (non-vocab params)^γ with γ≈0.83<1; verified predictions: ~3B non-vocab params → 39-43K optimal vocab, 7B → 62-67K, 70B → 212-231K (Llama2-70B's 32K vocab judged ~7x too small); raising a 3B model's vocab 32K→43K at equal FLOPs improved ARC-Challenge 29.1→32.0. Extrapolating the power law downward, a tens-of-millions-parameter laptop model wants a vocabulary in the low thousands-to-~16K range, else the embedding+head matrix dominates the parameter budget — with a math-only corpus (digits, operators, small English vocab) pushing it even lower.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13623>
- [S|medium|2025-09] Masked diffusion LMs work in severely data-constrained small-scale regimes: a BabyLM 2025 entry trained masked diffusion LMs under the 100M-word limit with frequency-informed masking (prioritizing rare tokens) and varied NELBO noise weighting, achieving 'performance competitive to hybrid autoregressive-masked baselines' — direct evidence that the architecture is viable at consumer-hardware training scales, not just at 100B+ token scale.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05056>
- [S|high|2024 (maintained)] MDLM's official repo is a working reference implementation whose config surface documents the architecture decisions: backbones 'DiT, AR transformer, and Mamba' (backbone=dit / hf_dit), parameterization=subs for the SUBS loss, optional time_conditioning=True kept only for the SEDD baseline, model=small with model.length=1024 context; it also implements a low-discrepancy sampler that reduces ELBO variance during training — a train-stability trick worth copying.  <https://github.com/kuleshov-group/mdlm>
TENSIONS: Time conditioning: D3PM/SEDD/DiT lineage builds adaLN time conditioning into the backbone, and DiffusionBERT (2023) spent effort on 'designs of incorporating the time step into BERT' — but MDLM's ablation (no perplexity difference), RADD's proof, and 2409.02908's equivalence result all establish it is unnecessary for ABSORBING (mask-based) diffusion specifically. The resolution: the number of visible mask tokens already encodes the noise level. Caution: this time-free property is derived for absorbing/masked diffusion; it does not automatically transfer to uniform-state or other corruption processes. || KV cache: LLaDA states flatly it is 'incompatible with KV caching', yet SMDM reports MDMs 'match ARMs in performance while being 1.4 times faster during sampling' using KV-cache acceleration, and the Kuleshov blog describes block diffusion supporting KV caching. Resolution: pure full-sequence bidirectional diffusion has no KV cache; block/semi-autoregressive variants restore it at the cost of some bidirectionality. || Sampling quality claims: 2409.02908 argues part of MDMs' reported generative-perplexity advantage comes from numerically inaccurate categorical (Gumbel) sampling at low float precision — a direct challenge to sample-quality claims in earlier MDM papers; any home-built evaluation should use float64 Gumbel or exact categorical sampling to avoid fooling itself. || Digit tokenization: single-digit tokenization has the best evidence at small scale (and is what Llama 1-2/Mistral/Gemma chose), but frontier models moved to 3-digit chunks for sequence-length efficiency and Singh & Strouse show the tokenization-direction gap shrinks with scale — so advice from frontier-model practice (3-digit chunks) is arguably wrong for a tiny laptop model, where single-digit is safer. || Vocabulary size: the scaling-law paper's headline ('larger models deserve larger vocabularies', Llama2-70B should be 216K+) is routinely quoted as 'use bigger vocabs', but its power law equally implies sub-100M-parameter models should use far SMALLER vocabularies than the customary 32-50K — the headline and the small-model implication point in opposite directions depending on scale.
GAPS: LLaDA-1B architecture table (Appendix B.2: layers/dims for the 1B variant) could not be extracted from any fetched version — only the 8B config is verified via HuggingFace config.json; the 1B numbers would be the more useful reference point for a laptop-scale clone. || Compute-optimal vocabulary predictions below 3B non-vocab parameters could not be verified from the paper's table (one extraction was internally inconsistent and was discarded); the small-model guidance (low-thousands vocab for a ~10-50M model) rests on extrapolating the γ≈0.83 power law, not on published rows. || DiffuGPT/DiffuLLaMA's specific adaptation mechanics (attention-mask annealing schedule, exact logit-shift implementation) were only confirmed at abstract level plus the Dream blog's 'shifted manner' statement; the step-by-step annealing recipe was not extracted from the paper body. || No primary source was found that studies digit/number tokenization effects specifically in DIFFUSION language models — all tokenization-arithmetic evidence (Singh & Strouse, Buckley) comes from autoregressive models; transfer to masked diffusion is plausible but unverified. || Quantified overhead of keeping adaLN/time conditioning (parameter count %, wall-clock cost) was not found — sources establish it is unnecessary but not what it costs if kept. || DiffusionBERT's conclusion about WHICH time-incorporation design won (including whether time-agnostic BERT was best) was not extracted beyond 'we investigate several designs'.

## Math datasets & data strategy for a laptop-scale diffusion LLM specialized in arithmetic/algebra/GSM8K-style problems (Evidence lens)
- [S|high|2021-10 (repo; verified 2026-07)] GSM8K is the canonical grade-school math benchmark: 8.5K human-written word problems (7,473 train / 1,319 test on HF), 2-8 solution steps, natural-language solutions with calculator annotations delimited by << >> and the final numeric answer after a '####' token — the parsing convention virtually every eval harness expects. Distributed as JSONL with 'question'/'answer' fields; an alternative 'Socratic' config adds auto-generated subquestions before each step.  <https://github.com/openai/grade-school-math>
- [S|high|2021 dataset; page accessed 2026-07] GSM8K is MIT-licensed and ubiquitous: the HF dataset page (openai/gsm8k) shows MIT license, 'main' and 'socratic' configs, and ~956,975 downloads in the last month — meaning heavy contamination pressure on any model that saw web data, but zero-friction availability for a from-scratch model.  <https://huggingface.co/datasets/openai/gsm8k>
- [S|high|2019 (v1.0); repo verified 2026-07 — historical relevance explicit (still the standard generator)] DeepMind's mathematics_dataset (v1.0, Apache-2.0) is a local Python generator producing school-level Q/A pairs across modules including arithmetic (pairwise ops, mixed expressions), algebra (linear equations, polynomial roots, sequences), and numbers (place value, rounding, primality). It ships 2 million pairs per module, questions capped at 160 chars and answers at 30 chars, with explicit train-easy/train-medium/train-hard splits 'which allows training models via a curriculum'. Generation runs locally via 'python -m mathematics_dataset.generate --filter=linear_1d'; pre-generated data is on a Google Cloud Storage bucket. This is the strongest fit for unlimited, contamination-free, curriculum-ready arithmetic/algebra data on a laptop.  <https://github.com/google-deepmind/mathematics_dataset>
- [S|high|2023-10-10 (ICLR 2024)] OpenWebMath is the open math pretraining corpus: 14.7B tokens / 6.3M documents (27.4 GB) extracted from Common Crawl with LaTeX-preserving extraction, quality filtering and dedup; ODC-By 1.0 license; top domains include stackexchange.com (9.55% of chars), nature.com, physicsforums.com. 1.4B-parameter models trained on it 'surpass the performance of models trained on over 20x the amount of general language data'.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06786>
- [S|high|2023-10; page accessed 2026-07] Full OpenWebMath-style pretraining (14.7B tokens at 1.4B params) is far beyond consumer-laptop compute; for this project it is a subsetting/mixing resource at best, while generator-based and small SFT corpora are the laptop-feasible path. (Inference from OpenWebMath's experimental scale vs. the project's hardware envelope.)  <https://huggingface.co/datasets/open-web-math/open-web-math>
- [S|high|2023-12-14] TinyGSM (CMU/Microsoft) is 12.3M GPT-3.5-generated GSM8K-style problems paired with Python solutions (~1.8B tokens). Finetuning a 1.3B generator plus a 1.3B verifier reaches 81.5% on GSM8K, beating the GPT-3.5 teacher (77.4%); before this, 'the smallest model size so far required to break the 80% barrier on the GSM8K benchmark remains to be 34B'. Key claimed lesson: 'high-quality datasets may be the key for small language models to acquire mathematical reasoning.'  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09241>
- [S|high|2023-12 dataset; page accessed 2026-07] TinyGSM is actually downloadable: HF dataset TinyGSM/TinyGSM has 11,846,109 rows, 3.75 GB parquet, MIT license, two fields ('question' string, 'code' string with Python solutions), ~1,280 downloads/month. Note the row count (11.85M) is slightly below the paper's 12.3M headline.  <https://huggingface.co/datasets/TinyGSM/TinyGSM>
- [S|high|2023-09-21 (ICLR 2024 spotlight)] MetaMathQA is a 395K-sample, MIT-licensed augmentation of GSM8K+MATH training sets using four bootstrap methods (Answer Augmentation, Rephrasing, Self-Verification, FOBAR backward questions), with the HF card explicitly stating 'None of the augmented data is from the testing set.' Finetuning LLaMA-2-7B on it yields 66.4-66.5% GSM8K (+11.5 pts over same-size SOTA at the time); 13B reaches 72.3%, 70B 82.3%. Secondary sources report the split as ~240K GSM8K-derived / ~155K MATH-derived (not confirmed on primary pages).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12284>
- [S|high|2023-07-07] Teaching Arithmetic to Small Transformers (10.6M-param char-level nanoGPT: 6 layers, 6 heads, 384-dim) gives the exact laptop-scale data-format evidence: on 3-digit addition, plain 'a+b=c' format plateaus ~85% even with 10,000 samples; reversing the output digits (least-significant first) reaches ~100% with ~2,500 samples via a sharp phase transition (1,000-4,000 samples); simplified scratchpad (digit sums + carries) needs ~2,000; detailed scratchpad needs only ~1,000. They also used balanced digit sampling (all 100 1-digit, 900 2-digit, 9,000 3-digit) rather than uniform random operands. CoT-style intermediate steps 'significantly improves accuracy, sample complexity, and convergence speed'.  <https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03381>
- [S|high|2024-02-22] Tokenization direction materially affects arithmetic: Singh & Strouse (DeepMind) show right-to-left number tokenization (enforced by comma-separating digits at inference) 'leads to largely improved performance' over standard left-to-right chunking in GPT-3.5/4; L2R errors follow stereotyped, systematic patterns; and 'the gap between tokenization directions decreases when models are scaled' — i.e., tokenization choice matters most at exactly the small scale this project targets. LLaMA/PaLM-style single-digit tokenization is the contrasting design.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14903>
- [S|medium|2024-10] Probing evidence supports digit-level number handling: LLMs internally 'encode numbers using digit representations in base 10' — reconstructing a number's value directly from activations generally fails while digit-by-digit base-10 reconstruction succeeds with high accuracy — supporting a digit/character-level tokenizer for a small from-scratch math model.  <https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11781>
- [S|high|2025-05] GSM8K-Aug is the de-facto training corpus for diffusion LMs on math: 384,623 training samples extending the GSM8K train set with simplified equation-only CoT (created by Deng et al.'s Implicit Chain of Thought work); DCoLT filtered it to 382,553 examples after dropping samples exceeding 64 tokens or with invalid format. The short (≤64-token) CoT sequences are exactly what fits fixed-length diffusion training on constrained hardware. Data is downloadable from the implicit_chain_of_thought repo (with 4x4 and 5x5 multiplication datasets, format '[input]||[chain-of-thought] #### [output]').  <https://arxiv.org/html/2505.10446>
- [S|high|2024-02 (paper) / repo verified 2026-07] Diffusion of Thoughts (NeurIPS 2024) established the diffusion-math training pipeline this project can copy: finetune pretrained diffusion LMs (Plaid 1B, downloadable from igul222/plaid releases; SEDD via Docker image) on 4x4/5x5 multiplication + GSM8K-Aug from the Implicit-CoT repo; Plaid 1B finetuning ran 120,000 steps at batch size 128. Per DCoLT's comparison table, SEDD + DoT reaches 53.5% on GSM8K-Aug, and search-surfaced reporting says DoT on SEDD-medium beats similar-sized GPT2-medium with CoT by ~10% on GSM8K.  <https://github.com/HKUNLP/diffusion-of-thoughts>
- [S|high|2024-07 (ICLR 2025)] iGSM (Meta FAIR/CMU, ICLR 2025) is direct proof that fully synthetic GSM-style data suffices for from-scratch small models: a GPT-2-like model pretrained only on the iGSM synthetic grade-school-math generator achieves 99% accuracy in-distribution and generalizes out-of-distribution to longer reasoning lengths than seen in training; model depth correlates with reasoning performance. The generator code is open at facebookresearch/iGSM, so unlimited fresh, contamination-free word problems can be generated locally.  <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.20311>
- [S|high|2024-05-01 (final 2024-11-22)] GSM8K scores are partially inflated by contamination/overfitting: on the freshly commissioned GSM1k (matched to GSM8K style, human solve rates, and step counts), models drop up to 8% vs their GSM8K scores, 'several families of models show[ing] evidence of systematic overfitting across almost all model sizes', with Spearman's r^2 = 0.36 between a model's probability of generating GSM8K examples and its GSM8K-GSM1k gap; frontier models show minimal overfitting. Data-strategy implication: never let augmented/rephrased test-adjacent data into training, and expect real capability to be below GSM8K-measured capability.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332>
- [S|medium|2024-10] GSM-Symbolic (Apple) generates GSM8K variants from symbolic templates and found every tested LLM (including GPT-4 and Llama 3) drops significantly when names/numbers are varied or irrelevant clauses are added, showing high variance across instantiations of the same question — evidence for building a templated, perturbation-based eval set for a math model rather than trusting a single static test set.  <https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/gsm-symbolic>
- [S|high|2024-02; page accessed 2026-07] Orca-Math-200K (Microsoft) is a drop-in alternative SFT set: 200,035 rows, MIT license, plain 'question'/'answer' string fields with natural-language solutions generated by Azure GPT4-Turbo via a multi-agent setup; card notes 'This dataset is not intended for use in educational systems or organizations.' - [S|high|2024-10-02] OpenMathInstruct-2 (NVIDIA) provides the best published data-strategy ablations for math SFT: 14M question-solution pairs (~600K unique questions) generated with Llama-3.1 models, released 'under a commercially permissive license'. Findings: excessively verbose solutions are detrimental to SFT performance; data from a strong teacher outperforms equally-sized data from a weak student; 'SFT is robust to low-quality solutions, allowing for imprecise data filtering'; and 'question diversity is crucial for achieving data scaling gains'. ## Real-time diffusion visualization UI — lens: How / practitioner
- [S|high|2025-02 (repo/demo added Feb-Mar 2025)] The official LLaDA Gradio demo (app.py, contributed by apolinário/multimodalart) is the canonical reference implementation for denoising visualization: a generator function collects per-step visualization states, then yields them into a gr.HighlightedText component ('Denoising Process Visualization', combine_adjacent=False) as lists of (token, color) tuples. Color scheme: masked tokens #444444 dark gray; newly revealed tokens colored by confidence (#FF6666 red <0.3, #FFAA33 orange <0.7, #66CC66 green >=0.7); previously revealed tokens #6699CC blue.  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA>
- [S|high|2025-02] The LLaDA demo decouples generation from display: states are captured first, then replayed with an artificial pause — 'yield history, vis_states[0], response_text; for state in vis_states[1:]: time.sleep(delay); yield ...' — governed by a visualization_delay slider (0.0-1.0s, default 0.1s). The animation users watch is a paced replay, not the live compute cadence. Exposed sampling params map directly to UI controls: gen_length 16-128 (default 64), steps 8-64 (default 32), block_length 8-128 (default 32, semi-autoregressive blocks), remasking 'low_confidence' or 'random', temperature 0-1, cfg_scale 0-2, plus positional word constraints ('0:Once, 5:upon, 10:time').  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA>
- [S|high|2025-04 (Dream 7B release; paper arXiv:2508.15487)] Dream 7B's Gradio demo implements the same visualization via a cleaner architecture: a per-step hook ('def my_generation_tokens_hook(step, x, logits): visualization_token_states.append(x[0].clone().cpu())') passed as generation_tokens_hook_func to diffusion_generate(). Rendering again uses gr.HighlightedText with 3-state coloring (#444444 masked, #66CC66 newly decoded, #6699CC already decoded) and a 'Visualization Delay (seconds)' slider 0.0-0.5, default 0.02s. Exposed params: max tokens 16-512, steps 8-512, temperature, top-p, top-k, and remasking algorithm selector (origin, maskgit_plus, topk_margin, entropy).  <https://github.com/DreamLM/Dream>
- [S|high|2025-04] Dream's generation API bakes visualization support into the model interface itself, so a UI never needs to hack the sampler: output_history=True returns 'the output at each intermediate step'; generation_tokens_hook_func 'can be user-defined to control the tokens at each intermediate step, e.g., print, infill, or other token control strategies' (demo_token_control.py); generation_logits_hook_func exposes per-step logits (enables confidence/entropy heatmaps). This hook pattern is the recommended design for a from-scratch build: emit (step, full_token_sequence, logits) events from the sampling loop.  <https://github.com/DreamLM/Dream>
- [S|high|2025-2026 (Inception API launched Mar 2025)] Inception's production Mercury API has a first-class 'diffusing' (bool) parameter documented as 'stream intermediate denoising steps' — the only commercial precedent for streaming diffusion states over an OpenAI-compatible SSE endpoint (example payload: {"model": "mercury-coder-small", "stream": True, "diffusing": True}). Official docs live at docs.inceptionlabs.ai/capabilities/streaming ('Streaming & Diffusion'); the page returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch, so exact chunk schema is unverified here, but the LiteLLM provider docs list the parameter and description verbatim.  <https://docs.litellm.ai/docs/providers/inception>
- [B|medium|undated (Mercury API era, 2025-2026)] Practitioner evidence on consuming a diffusing stream: intermediate states must be rendered as full replacements, not appended deltas. A hands-on writeup parses 'json.loads(line[6:])["choices"][0]["delta"]["content"]' and re-renders each frame with clear_output(wait=True) + display(text) in a notebook because 'diffusion streaming fills in the blanks'; the author also observed failure modes where 'streaming produced blank content' on complex tasks. Downstream tools default to diffusing:false (an openclaw feature request asks for passthrough support), showing OpenAI delta-append client assumptions break on diffusion streams.  <https://medium.com/@mrsirsh/quickly-trying-out-inception-mercury-e503d836753d>
- [S|high|current docs (fetched 2026-07)] Gradio's streaming model is generator functions that yield successive values, with a queue required ('demo.queue() ... required for generators'); the official guide itself suggests time.sleep() between yields 'so that you are able to observe the steps'. This is the exact mechanism both LLaDA and Dream demos use, making Gradio the lowest-effort path to a working denoising visualizer.  <https://www.gradio.app/guides/streaming-outputs>
- [A|high|2024 (Gradio 4 era)] Browser rendering, not model speed, is the binding constraint on 'real-time' visualization frame rate in Gradio: after migrating from WebSockets to SSE in Gradio 4, streaming updates 'would stack up in the browser's event stack and be prioritized over any browser repaint', causing lag; the fix (PR #7084) throttles pushes 'to a maximum rate of 20/sec'. Any Gradio-based denoising UI is therefore capped at ~20 visual frames/sec regardless of how fast denoising steps complete — plan step-to-frame mapping accordingly.  <https://huggingface.co/posts/abidlabs/528535313265224>
- [S|high|2023-10] Measured Gradio UI streaming ceiling from a bug report: the frontend could not sustain more than ~100 tokens/sec even when the backend was faster ('I easily see the backend finish twice as soon as UI even for only a single line of about 100 characters'), with Chrome pegging 100% of one CPU core during streaming; lag scaled with the number of static UI elements (300 unused elements added ~0.001-0.003s per update; workaround: minimize elements). Gradio 3.47.1. Implication for a denoising visualizer: keep the DOM minimal and expect frame drops if you push every step of a fast sampler.  <https://github.com/gradio-app/gradio/issues/5914>
- [A|high|2025-05-21] At production dLLM speeds there is essentially nothing to watch without artificial slowdown: Simon Willison measured Google's Gemini Diffusion demo at 857 tokens/second ('Build a simulated chat app' returned in single-digit seconds); his post describes output arriving as a finished artifact, with no mention of visible intermediate denoising states in the demo UI. Google's own model page claims 1479 tokens/sec average sampling (excluding 0.84s overhead) and describes generation as 'refining noise, step-by-step' that 'generates entire blocks of tokens at once'.  <https://simonwillison.net/2025/May/21/gemini-diffusion/>
- [A|high|2026-06-10] The research behind Gemini Diffusion shipped as an open-weight model in June 2026 — google/diffusiongemma-26B-A4B-it (Apache 2), free-hosted on NVIDIA NIM; Willison generated 2,409 tokens in 4.4s (~500+ tok/s) via a simple generate.py script. - [S|high|2025-02] The canonical explanatory visualization of masked-diffusion sampling (the LLaDA README GIF) distinguishes two layers a good UI should also separate: 'the non-white background represents the model's generation process, while the white-background boxes indicate the predictions made by the mask predictor at each step' — i.e., committed tokens vs the predictor's provisional guesses (which may be remasked). - [A|medium|2025-2026 (undated in fetch); RoBERTaDiffusion undated (2025 era)] Laptop-adjacent scale precedent with visualization: the HF blog 'BERTs that chat' turns ModernBERT-base/large into diffusion chatbots via dLLM by training 'across the entire range of mask rates (0-100%)', states 'training can be completed on a single GPU', and shows the denoising generation as an animated GIF demo. ## Hardware envelope & local constraints for building/training a small diffusion LLM on a consumer laptop — Economics / how
- [S|high|2023-04 (blog; math is not time-sensitive)] Canonical training-memory formula: mixed-precision AdamW training needs ~16 bytes/parameter before activations — 2 B/param fp16/bf16 weights + 4 B/param gradients (fp32) or 2 B (fp16) + 12 B/param AdamW optimizer states (fp32 master copy 4 + momentum 4 + variance 4). 8-bit optimizers cut optimizer states to 6 B/param; SGD+momentum is 8 B/param. Inference is ~1.2x model weights. So a 124M-param model trains in roughly 2-2.5 GB + activations — comfortably inside an 8 GB laptop GPU.  <https://blog.eleuther.ai/transformer-math/>
- [S|high|2023-04] Compute budgeting formula: total training FLOPs C ≈ 6PD (2PD forward + 4PD backward), and activation memory with full recomputation collapses to 2·s·b·h·L bytes (vs sbhL(10+24/t+5as/ht) without recomputation). This lets you size a laptop run: params x tokens fixes the FLOPs bill regardless of hardware.  <https://blog.eleuther.ai/transformer-math/>
- [A|high|2025-12-02] Best recent single-consumer-GPU pretraining anchor: a 163M-param GPT-2-small-class model was trained from scratch on one RTX 3090 (24 GB) in ~44-48 h on 3.2B FineWeb tokens at ~19,921 tokens/s using AMP + TF32 (22% speedup over fp32), reaching val loss 3.69-3.94; weights ~670 MB, optimizer state ~1.3 GB in checkpoints; total ~3.2e18 FLOPs. Extrapolating a laptop 4060/4070 as roughly 2-4x slower (memory-bandwidth- and TGP-bound) puts the same run at ~4-8 days; that extrapolation is my inference, the measured numbers are direct.  <https://www.gilesthomas.com/2025/12/llm-from-scratch-28-training-a-base-model-from-scratch>
- [S|high|repo, 2022-2024] nanoGPT's hardware ladder defines the consumer envelope: shakespeare-char toy (6-layer, 384-dim) trains in ~3 min on an A100; a shrunk config (4 layers, 4 heads, 128 embd, block 64, batch 12) trains on CPU-only in ~3 min to loss 1.88 with --device=cpu --compile=False; Apple Silicon --device=mps gives '2-3X' over CPU; full GPT-2 124M reproduction needs 'at least an 8X A100 40GB node' for ~4 days; Windows users must pass --compile=False.  <https://github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT>
- [B|medium|2023 (early PyTorch 2.0 era)] Naive worst-case single-GPU anchor: a user training nanoGPT GPT-2 124M on one RTX 4090 (PyTorch 2.0 nightly, batch 12, grad-accum 5, block 1024) measured ~5 s/iteration, i.e. ~35 days for the full 600k-iteration run, while noting the config seemed suboptimal. Modern tuned runs (see Giles Thomas finding) are ~15-20x faster per token, so treat 35 days as an untuned floor, not an expectation.  <https://github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT/issues/179>
- [B|medium|2026-05-08] On a Windows laptop with an 8 GB RTX 4060, the usable AI VRAM budget is realistically ~6.5-7.2 GB, not 8 GB, because Windows 11, browsers, and drivers hold GPU memory; and 'a smaller model fully on GPU is usually better than a larger model half offloaded'. Mobile 4060s are additionally limited by laptop power, cooling, memory bandwidth, and vendor tuning, so sustained performance varies between machines.  <https://knightli.com/en/2026/05/08/laptop-rtx-4060-8gb-local-ai-models/>
- [B|medium|undated (RTX 40 laptop era, 2023-2024)] Laptop GPU wattage (TGP) dominates real performance: the RTX 4070 Laptop is configurable 35-115 W (+15-25 W Dynamic Boost), boost clock 1230-2175 MHz depending on TGP, and a 140 W variant is only ~36% faster than a 65 W variant in 3DMark Time Spy (105 W vs 120 W differ ~1%). Two laptops with the same GPU name can differ 30-50% in throughput — training-time estimates must be per-machine, not per-GPU-name.  <https://laptopmedia.com/analysis/comparing-all-rtx-4070s-whats-the-real-difference-between-65w-105w-120w-and-140w-tgp-and-should-you-invest-more-money/>
- [S|high|2023 (driver 536.40+), still current behavior] Windows-specific VRAM-overflow trap: since driver 536.40 NVIDIA enables 'CUDA Sysmem Fallback', which spills GPU allocations into system RAM instead of OOM-ing. Training that exceeds VRAM silently slows to a crawl (VRAM ~hundreds of GB/s vs PCIe gen4 x16 ~25 GB/s per direction); the fix is NVIDIA Control Panel -> 'CUDA - Sysmem Fallback Policy' -> 'Prefer No Sysmem Fallback' so jobs fail fast instead of running 10-50x slow.  <https://nvidia.custhelp.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/5490/~/system-memory-fallback-for-stable-diffusion>
- [S|high|2025-10] Precision choice on consumer hardware: bf16 has fp32's dynamic range and needs no loss scaling — the 2025/2026 default on Ampere+ (all RTX 30/40 laptop GPUs) — while fp16 requires GradScaler-style loss scaling to avoid underflow (values <~6e-5) / overflow (>65504) and is mainly needed on pre-Ampere cards. Nuance: fp16 carries more mantissa precision than bf16, which one 2025 paper exploits to fix training-inference mismatch; for a small math-focused model either works, but bf16 is the low-friction default.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26788>
- [S|high|2025-03-05] Gradient/activation checkpointing trades ~one extra forward pass for large activation-memory savings: measured slowdowns across sources span ~22-33% for 50-80% activation-memory reduction (the exact percentage is workload-dependent and no single official number exists). PyTorch's Memory Budget API for torch.compile (experimental in 2.4) shows '50% memory reduction by recomputing only pointwise ops', and Selective Activation Checkpointing landed as prototype in 2.5. On an 8 GB laptop GPU this is the main lever to afford longer sequences/bigger batches.  <https://pytorch.org/blog/activation-checkpointing-techniques/>
- [S|high|current docs (2024-2026)] 8-bit Adam (bitsandbytes) cuts optimizer-state memory 75% (8 B/param -> 2 B/param) 'without losing any accuracy' and with no hyperparameter retuning; it helps parameter-heavy memory budgets but not activation-dominated ones. Integrated in HF Trainer via optim='paged_adamw_8bit'. Caveat for this project: bitsandbytes is Linux/CUDA-first and Windows support has historically lagged (medium confidence on that caveat).  <https://huggingface.co/docs/bitsandbytes/main/en/optimizers>
- [S|medium|2022-2024] CPU-only training is viable only at toy scale: nanoGPT's CPU path trains a ~1M-param char-level model in minutes, but a GSM8K-capable model (tens of millions of params, billions of tokens) is off the table — a single A100 needs ~10 h for even a 1-layer LM on real data, and CPUs are 1-2 orders of magnitude slower than that. Practical CPU-only ceiling for this project: <10M params, char/digit-level arithmetic tasks, hours-to-days timescale.  <https://github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT>
- [S|high|2022-12-28] The 'Cramming' study (Geiping & Goldstein) showed a BERT-class masked language model can be trained from scratch to near-BERT performance on a single consumer GPU in 24 hours, and that 'performance closely follows scaling laws observed in large-compute settings' even at this scale — evidence that one-GPU-day budgets produce genuinely useful masked-objective models (directly relevant since masked diffusion LM training is a masked-prediction objective).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14034>
- [S|high|2024-10-24 (rev. 2025-02-28)] The diffusion tax, quantified: masked diffusion models (MDMs) follow scaling laws with rates comparable to autoregressive models but need ~16x more pre-training compute to match ARM likelihood/generation performance ('MDMs with 16 times more pre-training time offer a flexible trade-off against ARMs'). Encouraging for this project: their 1.1B MDM was competitive with 7B Llama-2 on GSM8K math reasoning — masked diffusion punches above its weight on math benchmarks specifically.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18514>
- [S|high|2025-07-21 (v7 2025-10-26)] The diffusion tax reverses in exactly the laptop regime: in data-constrained settings with repeated epochs over limited data, masked diffusion 'significantly outperform[s] AR models when compute is abundant but data is scarce', because randomized masking acts as implicit data augmentation over token orderings; the paper derives new scaling laws and 'a closed-form expression for the critical compute threshold' where diffusion becomes preferable. A small fixed math corpus (GSM8K + synthetic arithmetic) trained for many epochs is precisely this regime.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15857>
- [S|high|2024-2025, actively maintained] Windows toolchain friction #1: torch.compile is unsupported on native Windows because official Triton wheels don't exist for Windows; options are (a) run with compile disabled (slower but works), (b) the community triton-windows fork (bundles TinyCC since 3.2.0.post13; PyTorch now points at it as fallback), or (c) WSL2. Plan for a 1.2-2x throughput loss if you skip compilation.  <https://github.com/woct0rdho/triton-windows>
- [S|high|2024-2025] Windows toolchain friction #2: pip install flash-attn routinely fails to build on Windows (hours-long compiles, C++ toolchain and CUDA/PyTorch version mismatch errors; Windows support only 'might work' since v2.3.2). The reliable consumer-hardware path is PyTorch's built-in F.scaled_dot_product_attention (SDPA), which needs no compilation and has fused kernels on RTX 30/40; community-prebuilt Windows wheels exist as a secondary option.  <https://github.com/Dao-AILab/flash-attention/issues/1469>
- [B|medium|2024] WSL2 is the lower-friction Windows path for CUDA training (full Linux toolchain: triton, bitsandbytes, flash-attn), with one architecture rule — the NVIDIA driver lives on Windows; never install a driver inside WSL2 (apt driver installs are the #1 breakage). Known tradeoff: WSL2 CUDA does not respect the 'Sysmem Fallback Policy', so over-VRAM allocations hard-OOM in WSL2 rather than spilling — arguably better for training (fail fast) but a behavioral difference to know.  <https://github.com/microsoft/WSL/issues/11050>
- [A|high|2025] Apple Silicon (MPS) works for small-model training (~2-3x over CPU per nanoGPT) with unified memory removing the discrete-VRAM ceiling, but carries hazards: unsupported ops need PYTORCH_ENABLE_MPS_FALLBACK=1; AMP/mixed-precision support is limited with no tensor-core-style speedup; memory pressure causes SSD-swap collapse; and MPS had a silent-corruption class of bug — addcmul_/addcdiv_ silently not writing to non-contiguous tensors, which froze Adam updates entirely (fixed in PyTorch >=2.4; macOS 15+ adds native non-contiguous Metal support). Minimum safe stack: PyTorch >=2.4 + macOS 15.  <https://elanapearl.github.io/blog/2025/the-bug-that-taught-me-pytorch/>

## Failure modes & limitations of diffusion LLMs (DLLMs/masked discrete diffusion) — lens: Failure modes, for a from-scratch laptop-scale math-specialized build
- [S|high|2025-08-01] Fixed-length generation is a first-class failure mode: standard masked DLLMs denoise a pre-set canvas, so output length must be chosen before sampling. On GSM8K, LLaDA-Instruct-8B scores 48.0% with a 64-token budget vs 83.8% at the optimal 1024 tokens (35.8-point gap from under-allocation), and over-allocation also degrades (82.6% at 2048) while wasting compute — at 2048 tokens only 14.4% of generated tokens on GSM8K are real content, the rest EOS padding. A builder must either sweep length per task or implement dynamic-length tricks (DAEDAL: EOS-confidence length estimation + iterative mask insertion).  <https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00819v1>
- [S|high|2025-05-28] Bidirectional (non-causal) attention makes DLLMs incompatible with standard KV caching: KV states change whenever any token updates, so every denoising step recomputes attention over the full sequence. Open-source DLLMs (LLaDA 8B, Dream 7B) are therefore slower than AR models in practice despite theoretical parallelism; fixes are block-wise/approximate caches (dKV-Cache, Fast-dLLM report up to ~27.6x throughput recovery).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22618>
- [S|high|2025 (repo current)] LLaDA's own authors admit sampling is slower than autoregressive models due to fixed context length, no KV cache, and — critically — that 'LLaDA achieves optimal performance when the number of sampling steps equals the response length' (NFE >= AR), with quality degrading as steps are reduced. The speed advantage of diffusion is not free; at equal quality the step count budget can exceed AR token-by-token decoding.  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA>
- [S|high|2025-10-06 (rev. 2026-06-23)] Parallel decoding has a fundamental (not merely engineering) failure mode: tokens unmasked in the same step are sampled conditionally independently, so joint-probability structure is violated (canonical example: 'She sell' from 'They sell'/'She sells' marginals). ParallelBench shows tasks with high conditional total correlation (e.g., Shuffle list ops, hard words-to-sentence writing) collapse from near-perfect accuracy at 1 token/step to near zero at high parallelism, while dependency-free tasks (Copy, Replace-index) survive; current DLLMs cannot adapt parallelism to task difficulty, so they fail to get meaningful speedup without quality loss.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04767>
- [S|high|2025-03-01] Irreversibility is a core sampler limitation: in standard masked diffusion, once a token is unmasked it can never be revised, so early errors persist and accumulate ('lack of error correction... sets limits on controllable generation, sampling speed, and sample quality'). Remasking samplers (ReMDM) restore revision ability and give inference-time compute scaling, approaching AR quality at high step counts — a builder should plan for a remasking-capable sampler, not just vanilla ancestral unmasking.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00307>
- [S|high|2025 (repo current)] A reasoning-specific sampler pathology documented by LLaDA's authors: with random remasking, intermediate reasoning steps that were already correctly predicted can get re-masked mid-generation, producing out-of-order or lost chain-of-thought; this motivates low-confidence remasking and semi-autoregressive block decoding for math tasks.  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA>
- [S|high|2024-06-11] A likelihood gap vs AR persists but is smaller than headline numbers suggest and partly a measurement artifact: MDLM (110M) reaches <=23.00 PPL on LM1B vs 20.86 for a matched AR transformer (~10% gap; MDM PPL is an ELBO upper bound, computed under the training-time distribution). DUEL's exact test-time likelihood shrinks the measured MDM-AR gap by up to 32% in-domain and 82% zero-shot; MDLM even beats AR zero-shot on some corpora (Lambada 47.52 vs 51.28) while losing on others (PTB 95.26 vs 82.05).  <https://s-sahoo.com/mdlm/>
- [S|high|2024-09-04 (ICLR 2025)] Evaluation trap for builders: Zheng et al. (ICLR 2025) show MDM training/sampling is theoretically time-agnostic (MDMs are 'secretly masked models'), and that the standard Gumbel-based categorical sampling has a numerical flaw even at float32 that silently lowers effective temperature, reducing diversity and inflating generative-perplexity quality claims — 'raising doubts about whether MDMs can truly beat ARs in text generation.' Their First-Hitting Sampler is 20x faster and equivalent. Implication: use float64 for Gumbel or FHS, and never trust generative PPL alone.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02908>
- [S|high|2025-11-22 (rev. 2026-05-21)] MDM training has inherently higher gradient variance than AR training from three stacked noise sources — masking pattern, masking rate (timestep), and data — vs data noise only for AR; symptom: MDMs and ARMs that are equally strong at initialization often diverge after task-specific training with MDMs falling far behind, plus large run-to-run variability. Variance-reduction fixes (Pareto-optimal timestep sampling, antithetic masking) recover 7-8% accuracy on reasoning tasks and bring run variance near AR levels. Expect noisy loss curves as normal; MDLM-style Rao-Blackwellized objectives and low-discrepancy timestep samplers are the standard mitigations.  <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18159>
- [S|high|2026-04-27] At small scale on precise, structured tasks — exactly the laptop-math regime — vanilla masked diffusion can fail to train at all: standard MDMs 'fail to reliably learn linear regression' (MSE stays at random level) and show divergent seed-dependent outcomes on graph path-finding, because isotropic random masking forces the model to average over many incompatible generation orders, turning optimization into stochastic search. Injecting blockwise left-to-right structure (semi-AR within blocks) restores AR-like trainability.  <https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24832v1>
- [S|medium|2024-10-24] Compute-efficiency gap at scale: Nie et al.'s first MDM scaling-law study frames MDMs as needing up to ~16x more pre-training compute/time than ARMs as the trade-off to match validation likelihood, though scaling rate is comparable and sampling can be faster (1.4x in their setup). For a laptop training budget this multiplier matters directly.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18514>
- [S|high|2025-07-21 (v7 2025-10-28)] Counter-evidence that conditions the 16x pessimism: in data-constrained settings (small unique dataset, many epochs — the realistic hobbyist/math-dataset regime), masked diffusion consistently beats AR in loss and downstream performance because it extracts far more value from repeated data before degrading; there is a compute/unique-data crossover below which AR wins and above which diffusion wins.  <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.15857>
- [A|medium|2026-03-23] Laptop-scale head-to-head (123-163M params, 50M TinyStories tokens): AR and MDLM training throughput are nearly identical (50,620 vs 48,343 tok/s on one H100, 4.5% difference), but convergence differs — AR hits best val loss at step 14K then overfits, MDLM improves monotonically without plateau; MDLM needed 31.6% more parameters, and its samples show occasional grammatical inconsistencies while AR shows severe mode collapse in openings (99.8% identical first word vs 36.1% unique for MDLM). Caveats: single seed, no downstream evals, one dataset.  <https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22075>
- [B|medium|not stated (fetched 2026-07)] Local CPU inference of a 7B DLLM is already demonstrated but uneven: diffuse-cpp's Dream-v0-Instruct-7B GGUF (Q4_K_M ~5.0 GB) averages 11.6 tok/s on an AMD EPYC 4465P with entropy-exit + inter-step cache; trivial math prompts ('15 x 23?') finish in 2 denoising steps at 21.6 tok/s (2.5x llama.cpp Qwen2.5-7B baseline of 8.51 tok/s), but creative/long-form prompts need ~16 steps and drop to 4.5-4.9 tok/s. llama.cpp master also ships a diffusion example supporting Dream, LLaDA, and RND1 with a default of 256 diffusion steps — so consumer-hardware DLLM inference is feasible, with step count the dominant cost knob.  <https://huggingface.co/diffuse-cpp/Dream-v0-Instruct-7B-GGUF/blob/main/README.md>
- [B|high|2025-03-07] The official LLaDA reference implementation itself is a pitfall: Issue #36 (2025-03-07) reports generate.py is too slow on GPU — unnecessary float64 ops, inefficient tensor ops, high memory use, and an excessive 128-step default — proposing fp16/mixed precision, fewer steps (32-64), and torch.compile; the issue shows no maintainer response. Builders copying reference DLLM code inherit these inefficiencies (note the float64->fp16 advice collides with Zheng et al.'s finding that low-precision Gumbel sampling silently lowers effective temperature).  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA/issues/36>
- [S|high|2023-06-27] Math-specialization failure mode independent of the diffusion-vs-AR choice: small transformers fail to length-generalize arithmetic beyond training digit counts. Relative position embeddings let addition generalize (5-digit training -> 15-digit sums) but fail for multiplication, which needs 'train set priming' (adding 10-50 long examples); later work (Abacus digit-position embeddings, position coupling) is required for robust digit-length extrapolation. A GSM8K-style model will silently fail on numbers longer than those in training data.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15400>
- [S|high|2026-05-26] Building the continuous/Gaussian-embedding variant of a text diffusion model is a documented trap: discrete tokens form isolated points in embedding space, the learned density becomes multimodal with low-density gaps late in sampling (critical interval t/T in [0, 0.4]), and DDPM-style solvers get trapped between clusters producing out-of-distribution outputs the model cannot denoise; mitigation requires self-conditioning (probability up to 0.95), alternative q-sampling solvers, and MBR reranking. Related work reports embedding collapse when embeddings are trained jointly with the diffusion model (Difformer's anchor loss). For a first build, discrete masked diffusion is the safer path.  <https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02028>
- [S|low|2026-07] Continuous-embedding diffusion LMs additionally exhibit a repetition failure mode: reported as a one-dimensional self-conditioning attractor where low perplexity manifests as repetition/looping in continuous diffusion LMs — a distinct degeneration channel from AR repetition loops.  <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.00588>
- [A|high|2025-11-04] Expert secondary verdict (Raschka, 2025-11-04): the DLLM speed-quality tradeoff is real — few steps is fast but degraded, enough steps for quality can match AR cost; parallel unmasking loses sequential conditioning (his 'New York City' -> 'New City' style example); diffusion models 'cannot use tools as part of their chain because there is no chain,' limiting agentic use; near-term niche is small on-device models rather than replacing AR LLMs. Aligns with ParallelBench rather than vendor marketing.  <https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/beyond-standard-llms>
- [S|high|2025-02-14] Contradicting evidence that DLLMs are not strictly worse: a 1.1B-parameter masked diffusion model breaks the reversal curse that afflicts much larger AR models (outperforming Llama-2 13B and GPT-3 175B on reversal tasks), shows competitive GSM8K math reasoning with Llama-2 7B, and LLaDA 8B surpasses GPT-4o on a reversal poem completion task — bidirectional conditioning is a genuine structural advantage for some reasoning patterns, including equation-style fill-in-the-middle.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992>
TENSIONS: Compute efficiency: Nie et al. (2410.18514) frame MDMs as needing up to ~16x more pre-training compute to match AR likelihood, but CMU/Lambda (2507.15857) show diffusion BEATS AR in data-constrained/many-epoch regimes, and the 2026 controlled small-scale comparison (2603.22075) measured near-identical training throughput with MDLM simply converging slower and not overfitting. The '16x' figure is regime-dependent, not universal — and the laptop/math regime (small unique dataset, many epochs) is precisely where diffusion looks best. || Speed: vendor/frontier claims (Mercury, Gemini Diffusion, ~1000+ tok/s on datacenter GPUs) vs the reality that open-source LLaDA/Dream practical inference lags AR models (Fast-dLLM abstract; LLaDA FAQ admits slower-than-AR, steps=length for best quality). Parallelism is a potential, realized only with heavy engineering (caches, confidence decoding, custom kernels) or accepting quality loss (ParallelBench). || Perplexity gap: headline MDM-vs-AR gaps are partly evaluation artifacts — ELBO is a loose bound under the training distribution (DUEL shrinks the measured gap up to 32%/82%), while conversely Zheng et al. show some MDM generation-quality claims were inflated by a float32 Gumbel sampling flaw that secretly lowers temperature. ## AR vs diffusion debate — lens: Controversies
- [S|high|2025-07-21] CMU study (Prabhudesai, Wu, Zadeh, Fragkiadaki, Pathak) found masked diffusion beats AR only when data is scarce and repeated: AR wins at low compute near Chinchilla-optimal, but diffusion keeps improving with repeated data up to ~100 epochs (fitted repetition half-value R_D* ≈ 500 for diffusion vs ≈ 15 for AR, i.e., AR gets value from only ~4 epochs). They derive a closed-form critical compute threshold at which diffusion overtakes AR for a given unique-token budget, and state the practical rule: 'if you are compute-constrained, use autoregressive models; if you are data-constrained, use diffusion models.'  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15857>
- [S|high|2025] The same team's project page undercuts a pure 'diffusion is special' reading: when AR models were explicitly trained with varied token orderings, at N=16 orderings 'performance approaches that of diffusion models' — evidence that diffusion's data-constrained advantage is largely implicit data augmentation over orderings, replicable inside the AR paradigm. Example downstream gap at 100M unique tokens: SciQ 68.67% (diffusion) vs 58.05% (AR).  <https://diffusion-scaling.github.io/>
- [S|high|2025-11-05] NUS/Sea AI Lab 'Super Data Learners' (Ni et al.) independently confirmed the crossover under 'strictly controlled pre-training settings': with limited unique data, DLMs surpass AR by training more epochs; crossover shifts later with more/higher-quality data, earlier with larger models, and persists across dense and sparse (MoE) architectures. Headline results: a 1.7B DLM with ~1.5T-token compute budget on only 10B unique Python tokens overtakes a strictly matched AR coder; a 1B DLM hits >56% HellaSwag and >33% MMLU using only 1B unique tokens repeated (up to 480 epochs tested).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03276>
- [S|high|2025-11-05] The pro-diffusion Super Data Learners paper itself quantifies the compute counterweight: 'During inference, given a fixed number of sampling steps, masked diffusion models consume between 16× and 4700× more FLOPs per task, with this gap widening as the target sequence length increases from 16 to 4096' (Section 7.1), and its limitations section concedes 'DLMs trade compute for data potential. The crossovers rely on higher train- and test-time FLOPs with bidirectional attention.'  <https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03276v1>
- [S|high|2025-10-05] Deflationary ablation (Gao et al., 'What Makes Diffusion Language Models Super Data Learners?') attributes the data-efficiency advantage dominantly to random masking of input tokens rather than anything diffusion-specific, and reports 'similar gains can be obtained through MLP dropout and weight decay' on AR models — i.e., the crossover may largely be stochastic regularization under multi-epoch training, not a new modeling paradigm. Marked work-in-progress.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04071>
- [S|medium|2024-10-24] The canonical compute-gap number in the debate comes from Nie et al. 'Scaling up Masked Diffusion Models on Text': MDMs follow a scaling rate comparable to AR with a 'relatively small compute gap' — widely cited (e.g., by the CMU authors in their AIhub interview) as discrete diffusion requiring 'roughly 16× more compute than autoregressive LLMs' to match likelihood in single-epoch settings; their 1.1B MDM beat 1.1B TinyLlama trained on the same data on 4 of 8 zero-shot benchmarks.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18514>
- [S|high|2025-12-11] ETH-affiliated scaling study (von Rütte et al.) complicates both camps: DLM scaling 'strongly depends on the noise type and is considerably different from ALMs'; uniform diffusion needs more parameters but less data than masked diffusion for compute-efficient training (better in data-bound settings); yet 'all noise types converge to similar loss values in compute-bound scaling.' They validated predictions by training a 10B-parameter uniform diffusion model at 10^22 FLOPs (largest known public uniform diffusion model) and argue DLMs can be competitive with AR at large scale — i.e., the 16× penalty is not a fixed law.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10858>
- [A|high|2024-09] Zheng et al. exposed that headline claims of diffusion beating AR on generative perplexity were artifacts of a float32 Gumbel sampling bug: -log(-log u) truncates at ~16.64 in fp32, biasing sampling toward high-probability tokens (a hidden temperature-lowering effect). Affected evaluations include SEDD (ICML 2024 Best Paper), MD4, and MDLM. With 64-bit sampling, generative perplexity jumps 31.24 → 126.11 and sentence entropy rises 5.17 → 5.66; the authors conclude MDMs 'do not actually outperform autoregressive models' on this metric and that MDMs are 'secretly time-agnostic' masked models (the continuous-time diffusion framing adds nothing over order-agnostic masked modeling).  <https://zhengkw18.github.io/blog/2024/mdm/>
- [S|high|2026-04-03] A 2026 Purdue position paper ('Generative Frontiers: Why Evaluation Matters for Diffusion Language Models', Pynadath, Shi, Zhang) argues most published AR-vs-diffusion comparisons are methodologically unsound: comparing ELBOs across models with different latent structures is unreliable; 'highly repetitive sequences can achieve competitive perplexity values while still corresponding to poor generative quality'; minor entropy adjustments can reverse model rankings; 'single-point comparisons measure inference settings; generative frontiers measure model quality.' Recommends entropy–perplexity frontier curves on OpenWebText instead of single-point generative PPL, and flags LM1B (sentence-shuffled) as unsuitable.  <https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02718>
- [A|high|2023-01-09] Sander Dieleman's canonical expert essay lays out why AR won for text: diffusion's vision success rests on noise weighting acting as a perceptual prior with no text analogue ('what does high frequency even mean' for text); AR training gets 'useful gradient signal from all steps in the sampling process' in one pass while diffusion trains one noise level per example; AR sampling caches activations while diffusion 'each sampling step requires a full forward pass across the entire input.' Concluded (Jan 2023) it was 'too early to consider diffusion as a serious alternative to autoregression for generative language modelling at scale' — the baseline position later challengers argue against. Historical but foundational to the debate.  <https://sander.ai/2023/01/09/diffusion-language.html>
- [B|high|2025-08] Hacker News practitioners attacked the 'super data learners' claim on methodology: godelski — 'I would be extremely concerned if people honestly believed that you could use loss as a strong indicator for comparing two different architectures'; thesz — the comparison 'matched DLM and AR by weights' not by compute; BarakWidawsky — apparent data efficiency may be 'Diffusion models having less capacity for memorization'; woadwarrior01 cited the '16× to 4700× increase in FLOPs' at inference; bicsi claimed iterated AR with chain-of-thought achieves equivalent results cheaper; counterpoint from fancyfredbot warned premature dismissal 'set AI back by decades' (re: neural nets).  <https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44856101>
- [A|medium|2026-02] Mercury 2's speed claims have partial independent verification but unverified quality claims: Artificial Analysis (independent, live measurements) recorded Mercury 2 at roughly 711–1,196 output tokens/sec depending on measurement window — described as over 3× faster than the next-fastest model in its price class — while Inception's own launch materials claim 5–10× lower end-to-end latency than speed-optimized models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, and Google. Even vendors position quality at the Haiku/Flash class, not the frontier; quality-parity claims rest on vendor-run benchmarks.  <https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/mercury-2>
- [B|high|2026-02] Firsthand HN testing of Mercury 2 (Feb 2026) found the speed comes with quality failures: Karuma got 'Dieadona' instead of 'Maradona' ('unforgivable mistakes' vs local 3B models); lprimeisafk said it 'fails the car wash test' (logic); espadrine caught wrong Unicode emoji facts it couldn't self-correct; nylonstrung: diffusion models have 'trailed the Pareto frontier for vast majority of use cases'; dmix: Mercury 1 was 'significantly slower than Cursor's autocomplete' in real use despite tok/s claims; Inception co-founder (volodia) countered 'Mercury is >5× faster at comparable quality.'  <https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47144464>
- [A|high|2025-05-21] Simon Willison's hands-on Gemini Diffusion test (May 2025) measured 857 tokens/sec building a working chat app in single-digit seconds, but he noted the experience 'felt similar to the Cerebras Coder tool' running Llama 3.1-70b (an AR model) at ~2,000 tokens/sec — undercutting the claim that extreme speed is diffusion-exclusive — and that Google's own pitch was only 'the performance of Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite at 5x the speed' with no independent quality benchmarks available.  <https://simonwillison.net/2025/May/21/gemini-diffusion/>
- [S|high|2025-05] NVIDIA's Fast-dLLM paper documents that open diffusion LLMs are often SLOWER than AR in practice: dLLMs 'lag behind autoregressive models due to the lack of Key-Value Cache and quality degradation when decoding multiple tokens simultaneously' — parallel decoding disrupts token dependencies via the conditional-independence assumption. Their fix (block-wise approximate KV cache + confidence-aware parallel decoding) yields up to 27.6× throughput on LLaDA/Dream with 1-2 point accuracy cost — proving the advertised dLLM speed advantage is an engineering outcome, not intrinsic, and quality-speed tradeoffs are real.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22618>
- [S|high|2024-10-18] Pro-diffusion evidence on reasoning-order tasks (ICLR 2025, Ye et al. 'Beyond Autoregression'): discrete diffusion (MGDM) dramatically beats AR on planning-heavy benchmarks — Sudoku 100% vs 20.7%, Countdown 91.5% vs 45.8% — attributed to 'subgoal imbalance': AR struggles to learn hard intermediate steps in fixed left-to-right order while diffusion can acquire easy subgoals first in any order. Directly relevant to whether diffusion architectures suit equation-solving/math tasks.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14157>
- [A|high|2025-10-20] Nathan Barry's viral post (Oct 20, 2025) demonstrated 'BERT-style models are essentially just text diffusion models trained on one masking rate' by fine-tuning RoBERTa with variable masking rates into a working 256-token generator — fueling the deflationary side of the debate ('discrete diffusion = generalized masked LM from 2018, rebranded'). It prompted a public response from Karpathy on X.  <https://nathan.rs/posts/roberta-diffusion/>
- [C|medium|2025-10] Karpathy's October 2025 reflection (as reported in translated coverage of his X post responding to Barry) stayed deliberately agnostic: 'Human thinking may be more autoregressive - a step-by-step process. But it's hard to say that there isn't a mechanism more like diffusion in our mental latent space'; 'Perhaps there's room for interpolation or further generalization between the two'; calling the generative objective the 'flexible' part of the LLM stack. He did not argue autoregression should end — frequently miscited as a stronger pro-diffusion endorsement than it was.  <https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3539549367824519>
- [S|medium|2026-03-23] A small-scale controlled comparison (Vicentino, Mar 2026; 50M TinyStories tokens, matched data/compute/hardware — the closest analogue to a laptop-scale build) found: masked diffusion training costs only +4.7% wall-clock vs AR at ~50K tokens/sec; AR converges faster but overfits by step 14K while diffusion is still improving at 20K; and a structural diversity-fluency tradeoff — AR outputs fluent but 99.8% began identically, diffusion gave 93.4% unique 5-word openings (higher Distinct-n, lower Self-BLEU) with occasional grammatical breaks. Single-author unreviewed preprint — treat as indicative, not definitive.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22075>
- [S|medium|2026-02-17] The any-order-modeling cost side of the debate: Pynadath & Zhang (Feb 2026) show any-order autoregressive models face a 'structural-semantic tradeoff' — each hidden state must simultaneously attend to semantically informative tokens for prediction and structurally recent tokens for summarization, conflicting goals that require two-stream attention (extra architecture/compute) to resolve; they frame AO-ARMs positively as 'a promising path toward efficient masked diffusion by enabling native key-value caching,' i.e., any-order generation is worth it only with nontrivial architectural surcharges.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16092>

## 2025-2026 advances & trajectory of diffusion LLMs (When / future lens) — what changed, what shipped, where the field is heading, and what it means for building a small local dLLM with denoising visualization for math
- [S|high|2025-03 (paper), repo active through 2025-2026] Block Diffusion (BD3-LM, ICLR 2025 oral, arXiv 2503.09573, Mar 2025) established the architectural pivot of the era: autoregressive generation over blocks with diffusion denoising inside each block, giving KV caching plus variable-length generation. This block-diffusion design became the dominant template adopted by nearly every later 2025-2026 system (Fast-dLLM v2, LLaDA2.0's block-level training, DiffusionGemma's 256-token canvas sampling). Code is public at kuleshov-group/bd3lms.  <https://github.com/kuleshov-group/bd3lms>
- [S|medium|2025-05 announcement; waitlist status still current as of ~2026-04 per secondary reporting] Google's Gemini Diffusion (announced May 2025) demonstrated 1,479 tokens/s sampling (with 0.84s overhead) and ~5x speed of Gemini 2.0 Flash-Lite, but as of mid-2026 it remains an experimental waitlist-only demo with no public API, AI Studio interface, or announced GA timeline — a signal that even Google has not solved productization of pure diffusion LLM serving.  <https://blog.google/technology/google-deepmind/gemini-diffusion/>
- [S|high|2025-08-04] ByteDance's Seed Diffusion Preview (arXiv 2508.02193, Aug 4 2025) set the speed record for code dLLMs: 2,146 tokens/s on Nvidia H20 GPUs — reported as ~5.4x same-scale AR models, ~2x Mercury Coder (1,109 t/s) and ~1.5x Gemini Diffusion (1,489 t/s) — while staying competitive on code benchmarks, claiming a new speed-quality Pareto frontier via a two-stage curriculum and block-level parallel inference. Weights were not open-sourced (API/demo only).  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02193>
- [S|high|2025-05 (arXiv); ACL 2026 acceptance] LLaDA 1.5 (arXiv 2505.19223, May 2025) introduced VRPO (variance-reduced preference optimization) to make DPO-style alignment work on masked diffusion models despite high-variance ELBO likelihood estimates, gaining GSM8K +4.7, HumanEval +3.0, IFEval +4.0 over the SFT-only LLaDA. Its acceptance at ACL 2026 (2026.acl-long.524) marks academic mainstreaming of dLLM post-training.  <https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.524/>
- [S|high|2025-09] LLaDA-MoE (arXiv 2509.24389, Sep 2025, Ant Group/inclusionAI) is the first open-source MoE diffusion LLM pretrained FROM SCRATCH at scale (~20T tokens): 7B total / 1.4B active parameters, surpassing LLaDA, LLaDA 1.5, and Dream, and matching Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct on knowledge, code, and math — the existence proof that from-scratch diffusion pretraining reaches AR-competitive quality, and (at 1.4B active) a realistic quantized local-inference target.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24389>
- [S|high|2025-11 preview; 2025-12-10 paper] LLaDA2.0 (arXiv 2512.15745, Dec 10 2025; Ant Group + Renmin/Zhejiang/Westlake) scaled diffusion LMs to 100B for the first time: LLaDA2.0-mini (16B) and LLaDA2.0-flash (100B total / 6.1B active MoE), built by systematic AR-to-diffusion conversion with a 3-phase block-level training scheme (progressive block-size expansion, full-sequence diffusion, reversion to compact blocks). Apache 2.0, 32K context; flash averages 79.32 across benchmarks vs Qwen3-30B-A3B's 79.47, with vLLM/SGLang support and community quantizations for llama.cpp, Ollama, LM Studio, and Jan. Recommended decoding: block_length 32, steps 32.  <https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI/LLaDA2.0-flash>
- [S|high|2025-10] RND1-Base (Radical Numerics, Oct 2025) was the largest open base diffusion LM at release: 30B sparse MoE (3B active) produced by autoregressive-to-diffusion (A2D) conversion of Qwen3-30B-A3B via continual pretraining on 500B tokens (bidirectional masking, layer-specific learning rates, up to 8M-token batches), scoring MMLU 69.6, GSM8K 80.0, MBPP 65.4, with weights, code, and training report all released.  <https://www.radicalnumerics.ai/blog/rnd1>
- [S|high|trend across 2025-09 to 2026-06] The dominant 2025→2026 training recipe shifted from from-scratch diffusion pretraining to AR-to-diffusion conversion: CoDA (Qwen3-1.7B base), RND1 (Qwen3-30B-A3B), Fast-dLLM v2 (~1B tokens of adaptation, ~500x less data than Dream's 580B-token adaptation), LLaDA2.0 (knowledge inheritance from AR MoE), and DiffusionGemma (Gemma 4 backbone) all start from AR checkpoints. For a from-scratch local build this means the literature's cheapest quality path is initializing from or distilling an existing AR model rather than pure diffusion pretraining; LLaDA-MoE remains the main open from-scratch counterexample.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26328>
- [S|high|2025-05 (v1) and 2025-09/10 (v2)] NVIDIA's Fast-dLLM line defined the inference-acceleration trajectory: Fast-dLLM (arXiv 2505.22618, May 2025) added training-free approximate KV caching and confidence-aware parallel decoding to LLaDA/Dream; Fast-dLLM v2 (arXiv 2509.26328, Sep 2025) converts AR models into block-diffusion with block-level caching, sub-block DualCache, and parallel decoding, reporting up to 2.5x speedup over standard AR decoding and 217.5 tokens/s at batch size 4 without quality loss.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22618>
- [S|high|2025-10] Ant Group's dInfer (arXiv 2510.08666, Oct 2025) was the first open high-performance dLLM inference framework, decomposing serving into four modular components (model, diffusion iteration manager, decoding strategy, KV-cache manager); on LLaDA-MoE it averaged 1,011 tokens/s on HumanEval — reported as 10.7x faster than Fast-dLLM and the first open-model demonstration of dLLM decoding surpassing vLLM-served AR baselines (Qwen2.5-3B at 294 t/s in the same internal test). Vendor benchmark on vendor model; treat multipliers with caution.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08666>
- [S|high|2025-04 through 2026-07] RL for diffusion reasoning matured in a direct line relevant to math specialization: d1/diffu-GRPO (arXiv 2504.12216, Apr 2025, NeurIPS 2025) was the first policy-gradient RL on masked dLLMs, improving LLaDA-8B on four math/logic tasks; TraceRL (arXiv 2509.06949, Sep 2025, ICLR 2026) added trajectory-aware RL with a diffusion value model, producing TraDo-4B/8B — TraDo-8B-Instruct beats Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct by +6.1% relative on math benchmarks and the series includes the first long-CoT diffusion model — with the open dLLM-RL framework (Gen-Verse/dLLM-RL) supporting multiple architectures. Trace-based on-policy distillation (TOPD, arXiv 2607.16872, Jul 2026) continues the line, letting SDAR-4B match RL-trained TraDo-4B on MATH500 (+5.7 static / +4.5 dynamic).  <https://github.com/Gen-Verse/dLLM-RL>
- [S|high|2025-07] 'Diffusion Beats Autoregressive in Data-Constrained Settings' (arXiv 2507.15857, CMU, Jul 2025, NeurIPS 2025) showed masked diffusion models outperform AR when data is limited and reused over many epochs (implicit data augmentation from diverse masking/orderings), while AR wins near single-epoch Chinchilla-optimal compute. This is the key regime result for a laptop-scale build: small-data multi-epoch training — exactly the local scenario — is where diffusion has a principled advantage.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15857>
- [S|high|2025-05 through 2026-07] A few-step distillation wave is collapsing dLLM sampling from hundreds of steps toward 1-8: DLM-One (arXiv 2506.00290, May 2025) demonstrated one-step distillation; Apple's FS-DFM (arXiv 2509.20624, Sep 2025) matches 1024-step discrete-flow baselines in 8 steps for long text; 2026 continued with trajectory self-distillation T3D (arXiv 2602.12262, Feb 2026), Infinite Mask Diffusion (arXiv 2605.10518, May 2026), and Multi-Mask DLMs with closed-form ELBO and consistency distillation (arXiv 2607.19686, Jul 2026). Trajectory implication: near-term local inference costs per token are dropping by an order of magnitude via distillation, though fewer steps also means less denoising animation to visualize.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19686>
- [S|high|2026-06-10] DiffusionGemma (Google DeepMind, announced ~Jun 10 2026) is the first open-weights diffusion LLM from a frontier lab: 25.2B-total / 3.8B-active MoE (8 of 128 experts + 1 shared, 30 layers) built on Gemma 4 and Gemini Diffusion research, generating on a 256-token canvas via block-autoregressive multi-canvas sampling with entropy-bounded denoising, adaptive stopping, up to 48 denoising steps, and temperature decay 0.8→0.4; 15-20 tokens per forward pass, >1,000-1,100 tokens/s on a single H100 (FP8), 700+ tokens/s reported on RTX 5090, 256K context, and it 'fits comfortably within 24GB VRAM' consumer GPUs when quantized. Distributed via Hugging Face, Kaggle, and Vertex AI; press and model card report Apache 2.0.  <https://deepmind.google/models/gemma/diffusiongemma/>
- [S|high|2026-06] DiffusionGemma's own benchmarks quantify the persistent diffusion-vs-AR quality gap at matched scale in 2026: vs Gemma 4 26B A4B it scores MMLU Pro 77.6 vs 82.6, AIME 2026 69.1 vs 88.3, GPQA Diamond 73.2 vs 82.3, MMMU Pro 54.3 vs 73.8 — i.e., the open diffusion variant trades meaningful accuracy on hard math/reasoning for ~4x generation speed. For a math-specialized build, expect the diffusion penalty to be largest exactly on multi-step reasoning unless block/semi-AR decoding and RL post-training are used.  <https://huggingface.co/google/diffusiongemma-26B-A4B-it>
- [S|high|merged 2025; current through 2026-07] Consumer-local dLLM tooling landed in the llama.cpp ecosystem: llama-diffusion-cli (examples/diffusion) runs Dream, LLaDA, and RND1 GGUFs with flags --diffusion-steps, --diffusion-algorithm, --diffusion-eps, and --diffusion-visual, which live-renders the iterative unmasking process in the terminal during generation; Ollama merged frontend support for diffusion models (PR #12125), and LLaDA2.0 quantizations are listed for llama.cpp/Ollama/LM Studio/Jan. This is direct proof that CPU/consumer-GPU inference plus real-time denoising visualization — the core of the planned project's UI — already exists as reference code.  <https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/examples/diffusion/README.md>
- [S|high|2026-02-26] The dLLM framework (arXiv 2602.22661, Feb 26 2026; Zhou, Chen, Tong, Song; github.com/ZHZisZZ/dllm) is an open-source unified library for diffusion language modeling — training, finetuning, evaluation, and deployment pipelines — that reproduces LLaDA and Dream, converts BERT-style encoders and AR models into dLLMs, and explicitly provides 'recipes for building smaller models from scratch,' making it the most directly relevant 2026 reference codebase for a from-scratch local build.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22661>
- [S|high|2025-08, revised 2026-06-04] The survey/curation layer is now mature and actively maintained: 'A Survey on Diffusion Language Models' (arXiv 2508.10875, v1 Aug 14 2025, revised Jun 4 2026) names efficiency, long-sequence handling, and infrastructure as the open challenges; its companion repo VILA-Lab/Awesome-DLMs tracks the ongoing paper flood (roughly ninety new DLM papers in Feb-May 2026 per repo-derived reporting), alongside an earlier discrete-diffusion survey (arXiv 2506.13759) and a dedicated efficient-DLM survey — meaning a builder in mid-2026 has consolidated, current maps of the field rather than scattered preprints.  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10875>
- [S|medium|2025-11-06 (funding); 2026-07-24 (Mercury refresh)] Commercial trajectory: Inception Labs (founders Ermon, Grover, Kuleshov) shipped Mercury as the first commercial dLLM in Feb 2025, raised a $50M round announced Nov 6 2025 (led by Menlo Ventures, angels including Andrew Ng and Andrej Karpathy), and on Jul 24 2026 released a scaled-up Mercury refresh claiming higher quality than Claude 4.5 Haiku at one-fifth the latency and under one-quarter the price ($0.25/M input, $1.00/M output, OpenAI-compatible API); secondary reporting describes a 'Mercury 2' diffusion reasoning model exceeding 1,000 tokens/s. The market slot dLLMs won commercially by mid-2026 is the speed/cost tier, not the frontier-quality tier.  <https://www.inceptionlabs.ai/blog/mercury-refreshed>

## Practitioner & community reality of diffusion language models (DLLMs) — what hobbyists, open-source maintainers, and hands-on testers actually experience when running, fine-tuning, and building discrete diffusion LMs, especially at local/consumer scale
- [A|high|2025-10-20] Andrej Karpathy publicly amplified how simple discrete text diffusion is ('Nice, short post illustrating how simple text (discrete) diffusion can be'), framing diffusion as parallel iterated denoising vs autoregression's left-to-right, in reaction to Nathan Barry's RoBERTa-diffusion post; secondary coverage reports he was tempted to 'train nanochat with a diffusion model' and mused that generation order is a 'flexible' part of LLM architecture  <https://x.com/karpathy/status/1980347971935068380>
- [A|high|2025-10] The post Karpathy amplified — Nathan Barry's 'BERT is just a Single Text Diffusion Step' — demonstrates that a fine-tuned RoBERTa generates coherent text with only a training-objective change (variable masking rates + scheduled denoising), validating that BERT-style masked LM is a single-masking-rate special case of text diffusion; this became the community's canonical 'diffusion LMs are simpler than you think' artifact  <https://nathan.rs/posts/roberta-diffusion/>
- [S|high|2025-11-10] Nathan Barry's follow-up tiny-diffusion is a character-level text diffusion model at 10.7M parameters trained on Tiny Shakespeare, presented as a from-scratch minimal implementation (secondary posts describe it as ~364 lines, ~80% code-identical to a tiny GPT with differences isolated mainly to generate and get_batch); its Show HN hit 172 points, proving laptop-scale from-scratch text diffusion is a done, replicated thing  <https://github.com/nathan-barry/tiny-diffusion>
- [B|high|2025-11-10] In the Tiny Diffusion HN thread, practitioners exposed ecosystem gaps and behaviors: gdiamos noted 'One year later and there is still no inference engine for diffusion LLMs,' another user said 'someone needs to make it work with vllm,' Simon Willison converted the matplotlib denoising demo into a terminal curses UI (a working example of real-time unmasking visualization), and the author explained confidence-aware parallel decoding as the fix for tokens that don't fit their region  <https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45876742>
- [S|high|2025] ash80/diffusion-gpt (258 stars) adapts Karpathy's baby GPT into an annotated 7.23M-parameter character-level discrete diffusion model on Shakespeare using a continuous-time Markov chain corruption process and discrete Tweedie sampler, with a GIF of the denoising process — a second independent minimal reference implementation at trivially small scale  <https://github.com/ash80/diffusion-gpt>
- [S|high|2025-2026] nanoLLaDA ('the most bare-bones masked diffusion language model on earth', ~500 LOC, 110 stars) documents that the entire architectural delta from GPT is one line (is_causal=False in attention) plus the loss CE(logits[masked], targets)/mask_ratio, and publishes a hardware table on 4x NVIDIA L4 (23GB): 20M params ≈ 2 min, 135M ≈ 2.3 h, 477M ≈ 4 days compute-optimal — the best public calibration of what small-scale DLLM training actually costs, implying single-laptop-GPU builds should target roughly the 10-30M range for fast iteration  <https://github.com/Lukas-Xue/nanoLLaDA>
- [S|high|2025-02] LLaDA's official GUIDELINES.md states pre-training is 'straightforward and simple. Starting from an existing autoregressive model training code, only a few lines need to be modified,' provides the exact forward_process + weighted cross-entropy loss code, a 1%-of-batches random-length trick for variable-length generation, and specifies that SFT differs only in not noising the prompt — the primary recipe practitioners are pointed to when they ask how to train one  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA/blob/main/GUIDELINES.md>
- [S|high|2025-03-05] In LLaDA issue #30, the authors admitted the released ecosystem does not reproduce their training: LLaDA-8B was trained on H800 80GB GPUs (batch size 4/card) with a private cluster-optimized framework, the public SMDM repo 'only share[s] the same loss function,' and they are 'not sure whether SMDM can successfully train an 8B model' — practitioners trying to retrain from public code hit config gaps and OOMs; the community answer became ZHZisZZ/dllm (2,653 stars, Sept 2025), a lightweight HF-Trainer fine-tuning framework for LLaDA/Dream  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA/issues/30>

## From-scratch build curriculum & pedagogy — lens: How / learning path (for building a laptop-scale diffusion LLM)
- [S|high|Lectures 2022–2024; page verified 2026-07-25] Karpathy's free 'Neural Networks: Zero to Hero' is the canonical prerequisite curriculum: 8 video lectures building from scratch in code — micrograd/backprop (2h25m), makemore bigram LM (1h57m), MLP (1h15m), activations/gradients/BatchNorm (1h55m), manual backprop (1h55m), WaveNet (56m), 'Let's build GPT' transformer (1h56m), and GPT tokenizer/BPE (2h13m). Stated prerequisites: solid Python and intro calculus. It contains no diffusion content — the diffusion leg of a DLLM curriculum must come from elsewhere.  <https://karpathy.ai/zero-to-hero.html>
- [S|high|2024; verified 2026-07-25] build-nanogpt teaches training craft via a deliberately clean git-commit history: it reproduces GPT-2 (124M) from an empty file, with each commit explained in an accompanying video lecture. The reproduction takes ~1 hour and ~$10 on cloud GPUs. Pedagogical model: walk the commit history sequentially alongside the video; scope is base-model pretraining only (no chat finetuning).  <https://github.com/karpathy/build-nanogpt>
- [S|high|Config verified 2026-07-25; deprecation notice Nov 2025] nanoGPT's char-level Shakespeare config is the proven laptop-scale recipe and the natural AR baseline before a diffusion swap: 6 layers / 6 heads / 384-dim, context 256, batch 64, lr 1e-3 decaying to 1e-4, warmup 100 iters, beta2 0.99, dropout 0.2, 5,000 iters (~3 min on A100, val loss ~1.47). The documented CPU-only 'baby GPT' variant (4 layers / 4 heads / 128-dim, context 64, batch 12, 2,000 iters, dropout 0) trains in ~3 minutes on CPU; --device=mps gives 2–3x on Apple Silicon. Note: repo carries a deprecation notice (Nov 2025) pointing to nanochat.  <https://github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT/blob/master/config/train_shakespeare_char.py>
- [S|high|Verified 2026-07-25] Primary-source hyperparameter defaults at GPT-2-124M scale, from nanoGPT train.py: lr 6e-4, min_lr 6e-5, warmup 2,000 iters, cosine decay, weight_decay 0.1, AdamW betas (0.9, 0.95), grad_clip 1.0, dtype bfloat16 if supported else float16 (autocast + GradScaler only needed for fp16), torch.compile enabled by default, gradient_accumulation 5*8 with micro-batch 12. Contrast with the tiny char-model config (lr 1e-3, beta2 0.99): 'defaults' are scale-dependent, and copying big-model schedules onto tiny models is a known failure mode.  <https://raw.githubusercontent.com/karpathy/nanoGPT/master/train.py>
- [S|high|Released Feb 2024; verified 2026-07-25] minbpe is the standard from-scratch tokenizer module for the curriculum: minimal, clean BPE code with three levels (BasicTokenizer, RegexTokenizer, GPT4Tokenizer reproducing tiktoken's GPT-4 tokenizer), covering train/encode/decode, paired with the 2h13m 'Let's build the GPT Tokenizer' lecture and an exercise progression. For a math-specialized DLLM this stage is where digit-tokenization decisions get made — though a char-level model (tiny-diffusion route) lets you skip BPE entirely at first.  <https://github.com/karpathy/minbpe>
- [S|high|2018, updated 2022; verified 2026-07-25] The Annotated Transformer (harvardnlp) teaches the 'paper as line-by-line code' method: an annotated Jupyter/jupytext reimplementation of 'Attention Is All You Need' where each concept is immediately followed by the PyTorch code implementing it (original 2018, updated 2022). It is the template for how to read the LLaDA/MDLM papers as code later in the curriculum, though it implements the full encoder-decoder rather than the decoder-only/bidirectional encoder a DLLM needs.  <https://github.com/harvardnlp/annotated-transformer>
- [S|high|2022-06-07; verified 2026-07-25] Hugging Face's 'The Annotated Diffusion Model' (Niels Rogge & Kashif Rasul, June 7 2022) implements continuous DDPM (Ho et al. 2020) on Fashion-MNIST 28x28: Gaussian noise on pixels, U-Net denoiser, timestep schedule, sampling loop. It contains zero discrete/text diffusion content. Curriculum implication: it builds diffusion intuition (forward corruption process, iterative reverse process, timestep conditioning) but its core machinery (epsilon-prediction, U-Net, Gaussian math) does NOT transfer to masked discrete diffusion for text — treat as optional intuition, not the implementation path.  <https://huggingface.co/blog/annotated-diffusion>
- [S|high|2025 (post-LLaDA); verified 2026-07-25] nanoLLaDA ('the most bare-bones masked diffusion language model on earth') is a ~500-line implementation of LLaDA-style masked diffusion with a tutorial.ipynb that runs the full pipeline from scratch: download data, train tokenizer, pretrain, generate. Training objective: mask random tokens, loss = CE(logits[masked], targets[masked]) / mask_ratio — the weighting makes it a proper ELBO. Sampling: start all-[MASK], iteratively unmask highest-confidence predictions; supports temperature, semi-autoregressive blocks, and classifier-free guidance. Tested at 20M/135M/477M params on 4x L4 GPUs (20M trains in ~2 min there; 43K–143K tok/s).  <https://github.com/Lukas-Xue/nanoLLaDA>
- [S|high|2025; verified 2026-07-25] The single most load-bearing pedagogical fact for the build plan: the architectural delta from a GPT to a masked diffusion LM is tiny — nanoLLaDA frames it as changing is_causal=True to is_causal=False in attention (bidirectional attention) plus adding a [MASK] token and swapping the objective/sampler. tiny-diffusion confirms: its diffusion and GPT implementations share ~80% of code (diffusion ~400 lines vs gpt.py 313 lines). The curriculum can therefore be 'build nanoGPT first, then flip it'.  <https://github.com/nathan-barry/tiny-diffusion>
- [S|high|Late 2025 (post-nanochat); verified 2026-07-25] tiny-diffusion (Nathan Barry) is the closest existing artifact to the parent project's goal: a 10.7M-param character-level masked discrete diffusion model on Tiny Shakespeare, adapted minimally from Karpathy's nanochat GPT, trained ~20 min on an A100 (vs ~10 min for its GPT twin at 5,000 vs 10,000 iters — roughly 2x training cost at equal scale). It generates in parallel blocks by iteratively filling masked tokens with confidence-based decoding, and ships visualize.py for step-by-step generation visualization — a direct starting point for the denoising-UI requirement. Size permits local/consumer execution.  <https://github.com/nathan-barry/tiny-diffusion>
- [B|high|Late 2025; verified 2026-07-25] Practitioner evidence from the tiny-diffusion Show HN thread: (1) masked diffusion requires pre-committing to a fixed-length masked canvas; the author argues varying-length phrasings plus confidence-aware decoding mitigate truncation; (2) the ReLU^2 activation was inherited from the nanogpt-speedrun → nanochat → tiny-diffusion lineage on empirical grounds, not theory; (3) commenters flagged the lack of production inference engines for diffusion LLMs (NVIDIA Fast-dLLM cited as early work) — meaning a from-scratch builder writes their own sampler, which is pedagogically fine but sets expectations.  <https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45876742>
- [S|medium|2025; verified 2026-07-25] Awesome-Diffusion-LLM provides the explicit two-notebook pedagogical bridge plus a maintained paper list: continuous_diff.ipynb (diffusion LM in continuous word-vector space, as an entry point) then masked_diff.ipynb (mask-based discrete diffusion LM in token space supporting text generation), inspired by LLaDA's guidelines — a deliberate continuous-then-discrete sequencing for learners, plus organized paper categories (discrete diffusion, efficiency/inference acceleration, CoT reasoning applications).  <https://github.com/yczhou001/Awesome-Diffusion-LLM>
- [S|high|2025 (LLaDA paper Feb 2025); verified 2026-07-25] The LLaDA training objective the curriculum must teach: sample masking ratio t ~ Uniform[0,1], independently mask each token with probability t, compute cross-entropy only on masked positions with a 1/t weighting — proven an upper bound on negative log-likelihood, making it a principled generative objective rather than BERT-style fixed-15% MLM. LLaDA-8B pretrained on 2.3T tokens. Both minimal repos (nanoLLaDA, tiny-diffusion) implement exactly this objective, so paper and toy code align one-to-one.  <https://github.com/ML-GSAI/LLaDA>
- [A|high|2019-04-25 (canonical, explicitly historical)] Karpathy's 'A Recipe for Training Neural Networks' supplies the debugging discipline layer of the curriculum: neural net training 'fails silently', so proceed staged — (1) inspect data extensively; (2) build end-to-end skeleton with sanity checks: fix seeds, verify loss at init equals -log(1/n_classes) (= ln(vocab_size) for an LM), init final layer correctly, overfit one small batch to ~zero loss, visualize the exact tensors entering the net, gradient-check dependency structure; (3) overfit ('don't be a hero' — copy a known architecture; Adam at 3e-4 early); (4) regularize; (5) random-search tune; (6) squeeze. Named common bugs: off-by-one target leakage in autoregressive models, view-vs-transpose batch mixing, clipping loss instead of gradients, copied LR schedules from unrelated domains.  <https://karpathy.github.io/2019/04/25/recipe/>
- [S|high|Current docs (PyTorch 2.13); verified 2026-07-25] Official PyTorch AMP guidance relevant to the laptop constraint: torch.autocast + GradScaler together for fp16 training (autocast alone for inference); expect ~2–3x speedup only on Tensor-Core GPUs (Volta/Turing/Ampere+), modest gains on older ones; matmul dims should be multiples of 8; and — critically for this project — small or CPU-bound networks may see no benefit from mixed precision. bfloat16 has float32's exponent range, is more overflow-resilient, and can eliminate the need for GradScaler (nanoGPT's default: bf16 if supported, GradScaler only for fp16). Implication: on CPU-only laptops skip AMP; on a mid-range laptop GPU use bf16 autocast if Ampere+, else fp16+GradScaler.  <https://docs.pytorch.org/tutorials/recipes/recipes/amp_recipe.html>
- [S|high|Book 2024, repo actively updated; verified 2026-07-25] Sebastian Raschka's 'Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch)' (book + rasbt/LLMs-from-scratch repo) is the laptop-first alternative/complement to the Karpathy path: main-chapter code is explicitly 'designed to run on conventional laptops within a reasonable timeframe' with no GPU required (GPU auto-used if present); 7 chapters (text data → attention → GPT architecture → pretraining → classification/instruction finetuning) plus appendices on training optimizations and LoRA, bonus BPE-from-scratch notebooks, LR scheduler/hyperparameter material, and a 17-hour companion video course.  <https://github.com/rasbt/LLMs-from-scratch>
- [S|high|2025; verified 2026-07-25] nanochat is Karpathy's current (2025) recommended successor to nanoGPT and the codebase both minimal DLLM repos derive from: a full pipeline (tokenizer → pretraining → finetuning → evaluation → CLI inference) in a minimal/hackable codebase; headline '$100 speedrun' trains a GPT-2-class model in ~1.5–4h on 8x H100 (single GPU works ~8x slower), and it explicitly supports CPU/Apple-Silicon via runs/runcpu.sh with much smaller models. Caveat for this project: its headline recipes assume datacenter GPUs; the laptop path uses its structure, not its scale.  <https://github.com/karpathy/nanochat>
- [A|medium|2023-01-09 (blog), 2023-06 (64x note); verified via search 2026-07-25] For the theory leg, Sander Dieleman's 'Diffusion language models' (Jan 2023) is the standard expert conceptual reading on why diffusion dominates perceptual domains while autoregression dominates text; in June 2023 he highlighted a paper quantifying a ~64x compute-efficiency disadvantage for (continuous) diffusion LMs vs autoregressive ones 'across all scales... a constant factor'. Expectation-setting for the curriculum: a laptop DLLM will trail an equal-budget AR twin in quality-per-FLOP; newer masked-diffusion work (MDLM 2024, LLaDA 2025) narrowed but did not erase this gap.  <https://sander.ai/2023/01/09/diffusion-language.html>
- [S|medium|NeurIPS 2024; verified via search 2026-07-25] After the nano-scale implementations, kuleshov-group/mdlm ('Simple and Effective Masked Diffusion Language Model', NeurIPS 2024) is the step-up research-grade reference codebase for the simplified masked-diffusion (Rao-Blackwellized ELBO) formulation that LLaDA-style models build on — the natural 'read the real thing' stage of the curriculum.  <https://github.com/kuleshov-group/mdlm>
- [A|high|2026-07-25 (synthesis of sources dated 2019–2025)] Synthesis — an assembled curriculum with every stage backed by a verified artifact: (0) training discipline: Karpathy Recipe checklist; (1) AR foundations: Zero to Hero lectures 1–7 (or Raschka's book if laptop-only/book-first); (2) tokenization: minbpe — or defer by going char-level; (3) laptop AR baseline: nanoGPT char-Shakespeare config (CPU ~3 min); (4) the diffusion flip: tiny-diffusion (char-level, has visualizer) and/or nanoLLaDA tutorial.ipynb (BPE-level, ELBO-correct, CFG + semi-AR sampling); (5) theory backfill: Dieleman blog → LLaDA paper → MDLM code; (6) specialize data to arithmetic/GSM8K and build the UI on the visualize.py pattern.
